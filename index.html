The recommendation: one DRC-01C reconciliation journey

Choose one narrow problem: a small-business owner receives an automated ITC-mismatch intimation and cannot connect the aggregate difference to the purchase records that created it.

Recommended problem

“Which records created this difference, and what should I take to my reviewer?”

Best delivery-risk trade-off, subject to approval

A trader receives a DRC-01C intimation. It shows an aggregate difference between ITC claimed in GSTR-3B and ITC available in GSTR-2B; resolving the cause still requires reconciliation against the trader’s purchase register.

Product thesis: do not simulate GSTN or decide tax liability. Build a transparent bridge from the aggregate flag to source records, a plain-language explanation, and a professional-review handoff.

Why now

  • Real, specific mechanism: DRC-01C compares ITC claimed in GSTR-3B with ITC available in GSTR-2B and requires a Part B response when triggered.
  • Correct division of work: deterministic code performs every match and calculation; the model turns validated findings into plain language and constrained draft text.
  • Buildable safely: structured synthetic ledgers and notices create a complete demo without GSTN access or real tax data.
  • Bounded enough to ship: one notice, one composite persona, two seeded causes, one review-ready output.

Why GST wins the tie

1 · Working build

The input and output are concrete

A notice, GSTR-2B extract, GSTR-3B summary, and purchase register go in. The exact difference, source rows, explanation, and review brief come out.

2 · Meaningful AI

The model handles language, not tax math

Deterministic reconciliation identifies causes and amounts. The model explains only those validated findings and drafts a short Part B explanation for human review.

3 · Honest scope

No fake integration or outcome is required

The prototype ends with a review-ready artifact. It does not invent a submission, payment, portal status, or successful tax outcome.

Why not lead with Cyber Crime right now?

The account-freeze concept remains the best-researched problem in the repository. It has recurring first-person accounts, exceptional emotional stakes, and a genuine release-side process gap. It should not be discarded.

But it is the wrong Round 1 starting point from zero code. Its proposed solution must be accurate across banks, cyber cells, RTI exceptions, NOCs, courts, jurisdiction, and a new Money Restoration Module that serves a different user. A rushed build could look like a legal-document generator acting out a fictional backend.

GST has a cleaner contract with the reviewer: “Here is the automated notice, here are the records that caused it, here is a transparent explanation, and here is a human-reviewable response draft.”

Evidence and source boundary

Verified mechanism Government GST guidance says DRC-01C compares ITC claimed in GSTR-3B with ITC available in GSTR-2B; Part B can contain a DRC-03 payment ARN, a reason for the difference, or both. A missing Part B response blocks a subsequent GSTR-1/IFF filing. Read the government functionality note.

Direct pain signal A dated CAclubindia thread records filers unable to proceed while the portal checked DRC-01C compliance and asking other users how to resolve it. This supports confusion and workflow blockage, but it is not yet a dense small-trader evidence base. Read the DRC-01C thread.

Evidence gap The repository has strong official mechanics and several practitioner/forum signals, but no structured interview with a DRC-01C recipient. The recommendation is driven partly by buildability, not evidence superiority.

Prototype boundary This project will never access GSTN, submit a filing, use a real GSTIN, or advise a person to pay or not pay tax. It produces synthetic educational support and draft text for qualified review.

Research for GST Problem

The evidence behind the proposed DRC-01C problem, separated into direct user and practitioner signals, secondary research, confirmed facts, interpretations, and open gaps. Research collected August 24–26, 2026.

Research question

Can a small GST-registered business understand and safely act on an automated ITC-mismatch intimation?

The research tested whether the portal explains the aggregate mismatch well enough for a non-specialist to trace it to source records, understand the consequence of not responding, and prepare a safe response for professional review.

Evidence standard

  • Confirmed: supported by government guidance or a directly accessible dated user thread.
  • Corroborated: repeated across independent practitioner or industry sources.
  • Inferred: a product conclusion drawn from the evidence, not a fact stated by a source.
  • Open: still requires an interview, practitioner validation, or stronger source.

Primary research — direct voices and observed behaviour

Here “primary” means first-person taxpayer or practitioner material found in public forums and posts. The team has not yet conducted a structured interview with a DRC-01C recipient; this is online observational research, not interview evidence.

Strongest direct signal

People are blocked by automated compliance checks without a usable diagnosis

A CAclubindia filer reported being unable to file GSTR-1 while the system checked DRC-01C compliance and asked the forum how to resolve it. A closely related DRC-01B thread records a filer seeing a compliance error despite believing there was no prior mismatch; the practical advice offered was to refresh and retry.

What this supports: the system state and next action are unclear even to active filers, and users seek diagnosis outside the portal.

DRC-01C filing-block thread · DRC-01B compliance-error thread

Repeated notice confusion

Recipients ask strangers whether they owe money, must reply, or need formal closure

Across dated CAclubindia threads from 2019–2026, filers ask whether a mismatch case closes automatically, whether penalty is mandatory, and whether a reply is required after payment. One reported a roughly ₹3 lakh DRC-01B allegation despite seeing no difference in the filed returns.

What this supports: the recurring unmet need is interpretation of the notice, evidence, consequence, and next step—not merely discovery of the form.

₹3 lakh mismatch thread · Penalty and reply thread · How-to-reply thread

Mechanism strong, direct voices thin

ITC mismatch is often caused by records outside the buyer’s control

The research consistently identifies late supplier filing, incorrect GSTIN or invoice data, period differences, and duplicate claims as mismatch causes. However, the accessible primary-research pass did not find a dense set of first-person DRC-01C recipient accounts describing invoice-level reconciliation.

What this supports: these are credible causes for a synthetic scenario. It does not prove how frequently each cause occurs for the intended user segment.

Access limitation

The online sample is practitioner-heavy

Reddit search and direct retrieval were blocked in the research environment, while full X threads were often inaccessible. The reachable evidence therefore leans on CAclubindia and practitioner reporting and may underrepresent small traders who do not post on tax forums.

Required follow-up: interview one DRC-01C recipient or qualified GST practitioner and manually review logged-in Reddit/X results before claiming the user need is fully validated.

Root-cause map

Data dependency

The buyer’s claim depends on supplier-reported records

A timing or data-quality failure upstream can surface as a mismatch for the buyer. This is a cross-party data-pipeline problem, not a cosmetic portal issue.

Explanation gap

The system flags a total, not the evidence chain

The comparison detects a difference but does not reconcile the trader’s purchase/claim workpaper to show which records explain it. Users must create that bridge themselves or pay someone to do it.

Workflow pressure

Unresolved compliance can block the next filing

The hard consequence increases urgency while the meaning of the mismatch and safe response still require judgment. That combination pushes users toward informal advice.

Synthesis: what the evidence does and does not justify

Supported
  • DRC-01C is a real automated mismatch-and-response workflow with a filing consequence.
  • Users and practitioners report confusion and blockage around DRC compliance and notice responses.
  • Invoice-level causes require records beyond the aggregate GSTR-3B figure.
  • A transparent synthetic reconciliation can demonstrate meaningful backend/process thinking without touching GSTN.
  • Calculations should be deterministic; an OpenAI model is best limited to grounded explanation and draft language.
Not yet supported
  • That DRC-01C is the most painful GST problem by prevalence or financial impact.
  • That the team has interviewed a small-business DRC-01C recipient.
  • That the synthetic causes or Part B wording are safe for real use before practitioner review.
  • That every mismatch can be classified automatically or resolved without professional judgment.
  • That the prototype connects to GSTN, files a response, determines liability, or produces an official result.

Working PRD — Notice, Explained

Draft for team approval. It is deliberately narrow enough for a credible Round 1 prototype and will need an explicit decision update before implementation starts.

One-line product

Trace a synthetic DRC-01C mismatch from the aggregate intimation to the purchase records behind it, then prepare a plain-language brief for professional review.

A citizen-facing, synthetic-data prototype for DRC-01C only.

Product principles

  • Plain language before tax jargon.
  • Show evidence before recommendation.
  • Never imply a real GSTN connection.
  • Human review is required before any real filing or payment.
  • Mobile-first, with 44px minimum touch targets.

1. Problem

Who faces it: small business owners and sole proprietors who are registered for GST but do not have a dedicated compliance team.

Failing moment: they receive a DRC-01C intimation saying ITC claimed in GSTR-3B exceeds ITC available in GSTR-2B. The intimation identifies an aggregate difference; it does not perform the trader’s invoice-level reconciliation against their purchase register.

What is hard today: the trader must connect a return summary, an auto-generated statement, and their own purchase records; distinguish timing differences from their own bookkeeping error; and prepare a reason for review before responding.

Evidence confidence: the DRC-01C mechanism and filing consequence are confirmed in government guidance. Practitioner/forum evidence shows confusion and blockage, but direct small-trader interviews are still missing.

Problem statement: A small-business owner who receives an automated DRC-01C intimation can see the total mismatch but not the source records that explain it, turning a valid automated check into an opaque reconciliation task they cannot safely complete alone.

2. Target user and demo scenario

Synthetic composite persona

Meera, independent wholesaler

Meera represents a small GST-registered business with occasional professional filing help but no in-house compliance team. She is a demo persona, not a researched individual; the team must present her that way.

Synthetic scenario

₹21,600 aggregate difference

A mock DRC-01C follows a GSTR-3B claim that exceeds the synthetic GSTR-2B. Reconciliation against a synthetic purchase register finds a later-period supplier filing (₹18,400) and a probable duplicate entry in the claim workpaper (₹3,200).

3. Main citizen journey

1

Open a notice

Meera selects a sample DRC-01C notice from her synthetic case. The first screen says, in plain language, “Your claimed credit is ₹21,600 higher than the portal record.”

2

See where the ₹21,600 comes from

She sees the GSTR-3B claim total, GSTR-2B available total, and only the purchase-register rows that explain the difference. The app never invents invoice-level GSTR-3B data.

3

Understand the validated findings

Deterministic rules identify the later-period supplier filing and probable duplicate. The OpenAI model explains those findings in plain language and cites the supplied demo invoice IDs.

4

Review what still needs judgment

Meera sees a neutral checklist: verify the duplicate in her books, collect supplier evidence, and ask a qualified professional to decide the real response. The tool does not tell her to pay, reverse, or contest.

5

Print a review brief

The prototype produces a clearly labeled mock Part B explanation draft plus the supporting rows and limitations. It is a handoff for review, not a filing, case-status update, or claimed resolution.

7. Requirements and acceptance criteria

Functional

  • A reviewer completes the notice-to-review-brief journey in under 60 seconds.
  • The rules engine proves ₹18,400 + ₹3,200 = ₹21,600 and links both findings to synthetic source rows.
  • The model output is separated from calculations and names only supplied demo invoice IDs.
  • The print view includes evidence, draft text, and the mock/review disclaimer.
  • If the model call fails, the deterministic evidence remains visible with an honest retry state.

Trust, accessibility, performance

  • The first screen and evidence load without waiting for the model.
  • Plain English appears before every tax abbreviation.
  • Touch targets are at least 44px and the journey works by keyboard.
  • No dark mode for Round 1.
  • Every mock, limitation, and safety boundary is visible inside the journey.

9. Risks and mitigations

Risk: the evidence is stronger for the mechanism than for this exact user segment.
Describe the recommendation as conditional, cite the direct DRC-01C signals that exist, and obtain one rapid practitioner review. Do not invent interviews or claim evidence parity with Cyber Crime.
Risk: it sounds like tax advice.
Use educational wording, show evidence and uncertainty, include “review before use,” and never tell the citizen to pay, reverse, contest, or not pay.
Risk: tax jargon overwhelms the demo.
Start with Meera and the amount at stake. Explain “credit you claimed is not yet visible in the system record” before showing GSTR-3B or GSTR-2B labels.
Risk: the model invents an explanation.
Keep matching, amounts, and cause categories deterministic. Send only validated findings, require structured output and supplied invoice IDs, and reject invalid responses visibly.
Risk: the prototype feels like a static lookup.
Make evidence groups expandable, let the citizen confirm what they want reviewed, and generate a tailored brief. Do not add a fictional timeline merely to create activity.
