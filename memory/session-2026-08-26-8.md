## What the GST research tab documents

### Research framing and evidence standard

- Research question: can a small GST-registered business understand and safely act on an automated ITC-mismatch intimation?
- Evidence is explicitly labeled as **Confirmed**, **Corroborated**, **Inferred**, or **Open**.
- The page distinguishes direct voices from official process evidence and from product conclusions.

### Primary research

The page uses “primary research” carefully: it means first-person taxpayer or practitioner material found in public forums and posts, not interviews conducted by Kriti + Gaurav.

Documented direct signals include:

- A CAclubindia DRC-01C thread where a filer was blocked while trying to file GSTR-1 and asked how to resolve the compliance state.
- A related DRC-01B compliance-error thread where a filer believed there was no prior mismatch and received only refresh/retry-style advice.
- Dated DRC-01/DRC-01B threads from 2019–2026 showing recurring confusion over whether money is owed, whether a reply is mandatory, whether payment closes the matter, and whether formal closure is required.
- A roughly ₹3 lakh DRC-01B allegation discussed by a filer who believed the filed returns showed no difference.

The primary-research limitations are visible, not buried:

- There is no structured interview with a DRC-01C recipient.
- The accessible sample is practitioner-heavy because Reddit retrieval was blocked and full X threads were often unavailable during the original research pass.
- The corpus does not establish the prevalence of each invoice-level cause for the intended small-business segment.

### Secondary research

The tab documents these process findings:

- Official government guidance confirms that DRC-01C compares aggregate ITC claimed in GSTR-3B with ITC available in GSTR-2B.
- Part B can contain a DRC-03 payment ARN, a reason for the difference, or both.
- Missing the required Part B response can block a subsequent GSTR-1/IFF filing.
- Practitioner and industry sources repeatedly identify late supplier filing, incorrect GSTIN/invoice data, period differences, and duplicate entries as mismatch causes.
- Because GSTR-2B is populated from supplier-reported data, a buyer may be affected by upstream supplier timing or data quality.
- Existing CA, GST Suvidha Provider, accounting, and reconciliation products exist; the proposed differentiation must therefore remain a narrow notice-to-evidence explanation for a small operator, not a broad GST filing suite.

### Root-cause synthesis

The research tab frames the problem as three connected process failures:

1. **Data dependency:** the buyer’s claim depends partly on supplier-reported records.
2. **Explanation gap:** the automated comparison presents an aggregate difference but not the purchase/claim-workpaper evidence chain needed to diagnose it.
3. **Workflow pressure:** unresolved compliance can block a later filing while the safe response still requires judgment.

### Supported and unsupported claims

The page explicitly says the evidence supports the real DRC-01C mechanism, recurring confusion around DRC compliance, the need for data beyond GSTR-3B to diagnose invoice-level causes, and the usefulness of deterministic reconciliation plus grounded model explanation.

It explicitly does **not** claim:

- DRC-01C is the most painful GST problem by prevalence or financial impact.
- The team interviewed a DRC-01C recipient.
- The synthetic causes or response wording are ready for real use.
- Every mismatch can be automatically classified or resolved without professional judgment.
- The prototype connects to GSTN, submits anything, determines liability, or produces an official result.

### Research follow-up plan

The tab records three validation steps:

1. One qualified GST-practitioner review as a build gate.
2. One structured recipient interview without collecting GSTINs, invoices, or sensitive tax data.
3. A five-minute comprehension test with a non-tax specialist using only synthetic material.
