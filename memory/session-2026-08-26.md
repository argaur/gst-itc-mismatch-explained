## Decisions made this session

Recorded in `decisions.md`: the Cyber Crime pick was re-confirmed through an adversarial two-agent process rather than a single opinion, and the working problem statement was reframed from "explain/diagnose the freeze" to "RTI as discovery + missing release-side state machine."

## What surprised me / patterns that emerged

- The grill agent's strongest catch was that "diagnose freeze type" quietly assumed the product would have access to data (which cyber cell, which case) that real victims never have. That's the kind of assumption that looks reasonable in a one-paragraph pitch and falls apart the moment a judge asks "where does this data come from." Worth watching for in future PRD drafts: any step that sounds like a lookup or classifier should be checked against what the user actually has in hand.
- Running `/prd-create` autonomously while Gaurav was away worked, but it surfaced a real tension in the skill's own ground rules ("Gaurav finalizes ideas, pain points, and the problem statement, Claude never invents or assumes them") against his explicit instruction to proceed without him. Resolved by drafting everything but marking every gate as open and every pick as provisional, rather than either refusing to proceed or quietly treating a draft as final. That seems like the right general pattern for "do X autonomously" instructions that collide with a skill's approval-gate design: do the work, don't fake the approval.

## Lessons learned

None rising to the level of `past-mistakes.md` this session, the autonomous-PRD tension above was handled correctly in the moment, not a mistake to avoid next time.
