# Platform Research: RTI Online (rtionline.gov.in)

Researched 2026-08-24 for the "Build What Moves India" hackathon (backed by OpenAI / Varun Mayya). Prototype must fix a backend/process failure on an Indian public-service platform, using mock/synthetic data only, no live government systems, no undocumented private APIs.

---

## 1. What the platform actually does

**rtionline.gov.in** is the Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT) portal for filing Right to Information requests against **Central Government** ministries, departments, attached/subordinate offices and statutory bodies only. Most states run their own separate portals (e.g. `rtionline.rajasthan.gov.in`, `rtionline.karnataka.gov.in`, `rtionline.delhi.gov.in`), and many states still have no working online portal at all.

**Core flows:**
- **File an RTI application** — citizen registers with mobile + email, picks a public authority from a dropdown, writes a free-text request (fixed character limit), pays a ₹10 fee (waived for BPL applicants) via net banking/card/UPI, gets a registration number. [rtionline.gov.in](https://rtionline.gov.in/), [FileMyRTI 2025 guide](https://www.filemyrti.com/blog/rti-online-application-process-in-india--simple-step-by-step-guide-2025)
- **Statutory timelines** — PIO must respond within 30 days (5 extra days if routed through an Assistant PIO), 48 hours if life/liberty is at stake, 35 days if transferred under Section 6(3).
- **Transfer under Section 6(3)** — if the PIO decides the information sits with another public authority, the PIO (not the applicant) is legally required to transfer the application within 5 days and notify the applicant. [RTIwala](https://rtiwala.com/content/rti-application-transfer-guide-pio-duties-under-section-63-explained/), [RTI Foundation of India](https://www.rtifoundationofindia.com/cic-section-63-only-authorises-public-authority-tr)
- **First appeal** — if refused, ignored, or given an unsatisfactory reply, applicant files a First Appeal to the First Appellate Authority (FAA) of the *same* public authority, within 30 days of the PIO's reply (or 30 days after the 30-day silence period lapses). [FileMyRTI](https://filemyrti.com/blog/rti-first-appeal-second-appeal-guide)
- **Second appeal** — if the FAA also fails or the applicant is unsatisfied, a Second Appeal goes to the Central Information Commission (CIC) for central authorities, or the relevant State Information Commission (SIC) for state authorities, within 90 days of the FAA order (or after the FAA's 45-day window lapses). Filed online at cic.gov.in for central matters (no fee). [righttoinformation.wiki](https://righttoinformation.wiki/guide/applicant/second-appeal/cic)
- **Status tracking** — a registration-number lookup form (`rtionline.gov.in/request/status.php`) shows current stage but is portal-specific and does not track post-transfer or appeal-stage state well.

## 2. Where it concretely fails users today

### a) Wrong-portal / wrong-authority filing has no recovery path, and money is lost
rtionline.gov.in explicitly states that applications meant for **state** public authorities (including NCT of Delhi) that get filed on the central portal are simply **returned, with no refund of the fee**. [rtionline.gov.in FAQ](https://rtionline.gov.in/faq.php) A first-time applicant has no reliable way to know, before filing, whether the body they want information from is a "central" or "state" public authority, or which of dozens of near-identical department names on the dropdown is correct — the RTI Act's own guidance says "applicants are not expected to know the internal structure of departments," yet the portal expects exactly that at the point of submission. [righttoinformation.wiki](https://righttoinformation.wiki/guide/applicant/application/how-to-locate-public-information-officer)

### b) Section 6(3) transfer is a "loophole," not a safety net
Section 6(3) requires a PIO to forward a misdirected application to the right authority within 5 days. In practice, "this loophole in the Act is being increasingly used by government departments to deny information to RTI applicants by passing the buck" — applications are forwarded even when the receiving PIO already has the information, or bounced down to subordinate offices that don't actually hold it, restarting the 30-day clock each time and burying the original request. [Greater Kashmir op-ed / moneylife.in via search summary](https://www.moneylife.in/article/rti-judgement-series-pio-can-transfer-the-rti-application-to-as-many-as-required/33058.html) There is no user-facing tracking of a transfer chain once it leaves the originating authority — the applicant just stops hearing anything until (if ever) a reply arrives from an unfamiliar office.

### c) Appeal backlog at Information Commissions is severe and getting worse
Satark Nagrik Sangathan's 2025 "Report Card on Information Commissions" (released October 2025, marking 20 years of the RTI Act) found:
- **More than 4,00,000 (4 lakh)** appeals and complaints were pending across 29 Information Commissions as of 30 June 2025. [The Week, April 2025](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2025/04/19/rti-system-crumbles-7-info-commissions-defunct-over-4-lakh-appeals-pending.html); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fover-4-lakh-complaints-and-appeals-pending-before-information-commissions-karnataka-alone-has-pendency-of-over-50000-cases-3228800)
- Commissions disposed of only **~1.8 lakh** cases against **~2.4 lakh** new appeals/complaints filed in the same period — the backlog is structurally growing, not shrinking.
- At current disposal rates, the **Telangana SIC would take an estimated 29 years and 2 months** to clear a case filed on 1 July 2025 (disposal projected in 2054); **Tripura SIC ~23 years**; **Chhattisgarh SIC ~11 years**. 18 of 29 commissions would take more than a year. [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2025/04/19/rti-system-crumbles-7-info-commissions-defunct-over-4-lakh-appeals-pending.html)
- **Six State Information Commissions were completely non-functional** for periods due to unfilled commissioner vacancies: Jharkhand, Himachal Pradesh, Telangana, Goa, Tripura, Madhya Pradesh. [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2025/04/19/rti-system-crumbles-7-info-commissions-defunct-over-4-lakh-appeals-pending.html)
- The **CIC itself returned 38% of the appeals/complaints it received** — i.e. more than a third of citizens who successfully escalated to the apex commission had their filing bounced back on procedural grounds rather than heard on merits. [SNS press release, Oct 2025](https://www.snsindia.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Press-Release-2025.pdf)
- Karnataka alone has over 50,000 pending cases. [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fover-4-lakh-complaints-and-appeals-pending-before-information-commissions-karnataka-alone-has-pendency-of-over-50000-cases-3228800)

### d) Vague / non-responsive answers, and Section 8 exemption misuse
- The three most invoked exemption clauses — Sections 8(1)(j), 8(1)(d) and 8(1)(e) — together account for **almost three-fourths of all exemptions invoked**, and study finds many of these rejections are "too broad, poorly reasoned, or applied to the wrong type of information." [search summary of PRS India / theleaflet.in](https://prsindia.org/theprsblog/rti-rejections)
- PIOs frequently cite "exempt under Section 8" without naming the sub-clause or the factual reasoning tying the clause to the specific record requested — a defect that is itself appealable, and one that Section 20 makes personally penal (up to ₹25,000 fine on the PIO) — but only if the applicant knows to appeal it and can articulate why the exemption doesn't apply. [righttoinformation.wiki grounds-for-rejection](https://righttoinformation.wiki/explanations/grounds-for-rejection)

### e) Retaliation risk for RTI users is real, documented, and escalating
- Since the RTI Act came into force in 2005, **95 RTI activists have been killed**, with **175 documented assaults** and **186 cases of harassment/threats** recorded nationally by the Hall of Shame database maintained by the Commonwealth Human Rights Initiative / attacksonrtiusers.org. [Hall of Shame PDF, humanrightsinitiative.org](https://www.humanrightsinitiative.org/download/Attacks%20on%20RTI%20users%20in%20India.pdf); [attacksonrtiusers.org](http://attacksonrtiusers.org/Home/InMedia/)
- This is the figure the "Gaurav" section of the team CLAUDE.md references for a different platform (~51 murders was an older/partial count for a related but distinct context); for **RTI specifically**, the correct current citation is **95 killed / 175 assaulted / 186 harassed since 2005** per the CHRI Hall of Shame tracker — use this number, not the 51-murders figure, for RTI-specific claims.
- Recent, dated 2024-2025 incidents: **Sunil Kapoor**, an RTI activist in Jind, Haryana, who used RTI queries to expose police corruption, was shot and died on **14 September 2025** after facing multiple prior assaults and threats. [thefederal.com](https://thefederal.com/analysis/murders-attacks-take-wind-out-of-whistles-rti-activists-in-fear); [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunil_Kapoor)
- A differently-abled RTI activist in Gujarat was abducted on **12 October 2025** from Tharad district and found dead in a canal on **14 October 2025**; NHRC issued notice to Gujarat officials over the death. [newsonair.gov.in](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/nhrc-issues-notice-to-gujarat-officials-over-death-of-differently-abled-rti-activist)
- At least **11 RTI activists killed in Bihar** and **16 in Maharashtra** (with 36 more assaults, 41 harassment cases) in the past two decades, per regional breakdowns of the same tracker. [thefederal.com](https://thefederal.com/analysis/murders-attacks-take-wind-out-of-whistles-rti-activists-in-fear)
- **Caveat/flag:** exact yearly attack counts for 2024-2026 specifically (as opposed to cumulative since 2005) were not found in a single consolidated source during this research pass; the cumulative "95 killed since 2005" figure is well-sourced, the year-by-year trend is not. Treat any "attacks are rising" framing as directionally supported by named 2025 incidents, not as a verified statistical trend.

### f) Poor drafting by first-time applicants leads directly to rejection or non-answers
Guidance from multiple RTI-assistance sources converges on the same failure pattern: applicants who write questions rather than requests get rejected or stonewalled. Specifically:
- Questions starting with **"why," "how," "where"** are treated as seeking an opinion/justification, not a record, and are liable to rejection — the correct form is a request for "the record of the decision taken... in respect of X," not "why was X decided." [search summary](https://blog.ipleaders.in/drafting-an-rti-application/)
- Mixing multiple departments/subjects in one application forces PIOs to invoke Section 6(3) transfer (see 2b above) instead of answering directly.
- Vague requests ("all information about...") give PIOs an easy excuse to give a vague or non-responsive reply, since there's no specific document/record identified to point to.
- Best practice is to phrase every line item as "certified copy of," "file noting on," or "status as on date of" — i.e., a specific document, not a narrative question. [righttoinformation.wiki templates](https://righttoinformation.wiki/templates/first-rti)

## 3. Root causes — is this UI-level or backend/process-level?

This is squarely a **backend/process failure**, not a reskin opportunity:

1. **Routing is a one-shot, unrecoverable dropdown decision.** The portal asks a citizen who by definition doesn't know government structure to correctly classify their own request (central vs. state authority, correct department) *before* submission, with a punitive failure mode (application returned, fee lost, zero appeal path for a misfiled submission). This is a process design flaw: the system should resolve "what does this citizen actually need and who holds it" *before* money changes hands, not after.
2. **Section 6(3) transfer has no applicant-visible state machine.** Once a PIO transfers an application, the original registration number effectively goes dark to the applicant. There's no cross-authority tracking, no SLA enforcement visible to the citizen, and no way to know if a transfer was legitimate (info genuinely elsewhere) or a delay tactic (application "passed the buck" to a subordinate office). This is a tracking/orchestration problem, not a screen problem.
3. **Drafting quality directly determines legal outcome**, and the portal provides zero structured guidance at write-time — just a blank text box. The Act's own case law shows that a "why/how/where" phrasing versus a "certified copy of X" phrasing changes whether the request is even answerable under the law. This is a process-shaping problem: the input format itself needs to constrain citizens toward RTI-Act-compliant requests, not just collect free text.
4. **Appeal escalation (first appeal → second appeal → Commission) is a pipeline with no visibility, no reminders, and structurally guaranteed multi-year backlogs.** A citizen who wins the legal right to escalate is handed into a queue that (per SNS's own modeling) will not be reached for 11-29 years in several states. That's not fixable by better UI on the existing appeal form; it needs either smarter triage before filing (e.g., catching preventable Section 8 misuse or drafting defects before the citizen even needs to appeal) or a way to generate a properly-formatted, non-returnable appeal so it survives first contact with the commission rather than becoming one of the 38% CIC returns.

## 4. What's already been tried, and why gaps remain

- **RTI drafting guides** (RTIwala, FileMyRTI, RTI Foundation of India, righttoinformation.wiki, ipleaders.in) — all publish static "how to write a good RTI" articles with dos/don'ts. Useful but passive: a first-time filer has to already know to go looking for this content, read it, and correctly self-apply it to their specific situation. None of these tools validate a citizen's actual drafted text against the known rejection patterns before submission.
- **AI RTI drafters** — at least one (righttoinformation.wiki's "AI RTI Drafter") already exists as a free generator. This validates that AI-assisted drafting is a recognized gap, but a generic drafter doesn't solve the authority-routing problem or the transfer-tracking problem — it only touches step 1 of a multi-step pipeline.
- **NGO-run assistance — Satark Nagrik Sangathan (SNS)** — SNS's primary output is *aggregate accountability research* (the annual Information Commission Report Card), not individual applicant assistance at scale. Their work documents the systemic failure (pendency, defunct commissions, CIC's 38% return rate) authoritatively, but does not give an individual first-time filer a tool to avoid becoming one of those statistics. [snsindia.org](https://www.snsindia.org/rti-assessments/)
- **Gap that remains:** no existing tool connects (a) authority/routing resolution, (b) drafting-quality validation against known rejection patterns, and (c) transfer/appeal-stage tracking into one flow. Each piece has been separately, partially addressed; the seams between them (mis-filed application → lost fee; vague draft → non-answer; transfer → silence; appeal → commission backlog) are where citizens actually get lost, and that's unaddressed.

## 5. Buildathon fit assessment

**Verdict: strong fit, fully buildable in ~3 days on synthetic data, and squarely backend/process, not a reskin.**

- No live government system needs to be touched. rtionline.gov.in, cic.gov.in, and state SIC portals can all be treated as black boxes; the prototype only needs to simulate their *behavior* (routing rules, transfer mechanics, appeal timelines, rejection patterns), which is documented in public law (RTI Act 2005, RTI Rules 2012) and in the case-law/guidance sources cited above — not scraped or reverse-engineered from the live sites.
- No Aadhaar/PAN/OTP/payment data needed. The ₹10 fee flow can be entirely mocked.
- Mock datasets needed:
  1. **Synthetic public-authority directory** — a small labeled dataset of departments/authorities (central vs. state, with a few deliberately confusing near-duplicate names) mapped to the kind of information each holds. This powers an "authority resolver" that takes a citizen's plain-language problem and suggests the correct authority *before* submission, mirroring the actual gap identified in 3(1).
  2. **Synthetic application/transfer/appeal status timeline** — a set of mock RTI case records moving through states (filed → PIO reply/silence → transferred → first appeal → second appeal → commission queue), with synthetic dates, to demo a unified tracker that survives a Section 6(3) transfer instead of going dark.
  3. **Sample RTI request text pairs, labeled well-drafted vs. likely-to-be-rejected** — built directly from the documented rejection patterns above (why/how/where phrasing, multi-subject requests, vague "all information about" requests, missing specific-document framing) so an LLM-based drafting assistant can be demoed rewriting a bad draft into a compliant, single-subject, document-specific request.
- The valuable/demo-able part is the **reasoning layer** — resolving the right authority, validating/rewriting a draft against known legal rejection patterns, and tracking a case across a transfer or appeal boundary — not any live data feed. This matches the brief's requirement to fix the backend/process, not redesign the screen.

## 6. Candidate "fix the failing moment" problem statements

> **"You filed against the wrong department, and you just lost your ₹10 and your 30 days."** rtionline.gov.in only serves *central* public authorities; the portal's own FAQ states that any application meant for a *state* authority — including Delhi's own NCT government — filed through it will simply be returned, with **no refund of the fee**. A first-time citizen has no way to know, at the point of picking a name off a dropdown, whether "Municipal Corporation of Delhi" is a central or state body, or which of several similarly-named departments actually holds the record they want — even though the RTI Act's own principle is that "applicants are not expected to know the internal structure of departments." The result is a dead-end: silent rejection, lost money, and the citizen left to start over with no explanation of what went wrong. Strong buildathon fit: a synthetic authority directory (central/state split, with deliberately confusing near-duplicate names) plus a plain-language "what do you need and from whom" resolver is fully mockable, and the valuable part — correctly routing an ambiguous citizen request to the right authority *before* submission — is a reasoning/classification problem, not a UI problem.

> **"They said they transferred my RTI. That was eight months ago."** Section 6(3) requires a Public Information Officer to forward a misdirected application to the correct authority within 5 days and notify the applicant — but in practice this "loophole" is used to pass the buck, with applications bounced to subordinate offices that don't actually hold the information, each restarting the clock, while the applicant's original registration number goes dark with no visibility into where the request currently sits or whether the transfer was even valid. There is no cross-authority tracking once an application leaves its original portal. Strong buildathon fit: a synthetic multi-hop transfer timeline (filed → transferred → transferred again → silence) lets the prototype demo a unified tracker plus an SLA-violation detector that tells the citizen the exact moment a transfer became unlawful delay and what to do next (file a complaint, not wait indefinitely).

> **"My RTI got a one-line 'exempt under Section 8' reply, and I don't know if that's even legal."** The three most-invoked exemption clauses (8(1)(j), 8(1)(d), 8(1)(e)) account for nearly three-quarters of all rejections, and a large share are "too broad, poorly reasoned, or applied to the wrong type of information" — a PIO citing "Section 8" without the specific sub-clause and factual reasoning is itself a defect that's appealable under Section 20 (personally penal up to ₹25,000), but only if the citizen recognizes the defect and can correctly frame the appeal. Most first-time filers have no way to know a vague exemption citation is actually invalid. Good buildathon fit: a labeled dataset of real-shaped exemption citations (valid vs. invalid/vague) lets the prototype demo a "is this refusal actually lawful?" checker that decodes a PIO's rejection letter into plain language and drafts the specific first-appeal grounds — turning a citizen's confusion into an actionable, correctly-targeted appeal instead of a dead end.

> **"I want to escalate, but the queue ahead of me is 29 years long — did anyone tell me that?"** Satark Nagrik Sangathan's October 2025 Report Card found more than 4 lakh appeals/complaints pending across 29 Information Commissions as of June 2025, disposal running at roughly 1.8 lakh against 2.4 lakh new filings a year, and at current rates the Telangana SIC alone would take an estimated 29 years 2 months to clear a case filed 1 July 2025 (Tripura ~23 years, Chhattisgarh ~11 years); six SICs were entirely non-functional for stretches due to unfilled commissioner seats, and the CIC itself returned 38% of what it received on procedural grounds. A citizen filing a second appeal today has no way to know whether they're escalating into a commission that will actually hear them this decade, or one of the defunct/backlogged ones, and no guidance on what else is available (RTI Rules complaint route, media/NGO escalation, re-filing a cleaner request) while they wait. Medium-strong buildathon fit: fully mockable with a synthetic commission-pendency dataset (mirroring SNS's real published numbers) driving a "realistic wait-time and alternative-path" estimator, plus a second-appeal drafting assistant that avoids the specific procedural defects that cause the CIC's 38% return rate — this is the sharpest "moment" story because it's the point where the system itself, not just one clerk, is failing the citizen.

> **"I don't know how to ask for this without it getting rejected."** RTI guidance sources converge on a consistent, well-documented failure pattern for first-time drafters: questions phrased as "why/how/where" are treated as seeking opinion or justification rather than a record and are liable to rejection (the lawful form is "the record of the decision taken... in respect of X"); multi-subject applications force a Section 6(3) transfer instead of a direct answer; and vague "all information about..." requests give PIOs an easy excuse for a non-responsive reply since no specific document is named. Existing drafting guides are static articles a citizen has to already know to seek out, and at least one generic AI RTI drafter already exists, but none validate a citizen's actual draft against these known, legally-grounded rejection patterns before they spend their ₹10 and 30 days on a request that was doomed from the wording. Weaker as a standalone story since it only touches the first step of the pipeline, but strong as a component: a labeled dataset of well-drafted vs. likely-to-be-rejected RTI text pairs (built directly from the documented patterns above) lets the prototype demo a rewrite-and-explain assistant — this pairs naturally with candidate 1 (authority resolver) as two halves of one "get your RTI filed right the first time" flow.
