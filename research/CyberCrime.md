# Platform Research: National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in)

Research date: 2026-08-24. All figures cited inline with source and date; anything not independently verifiable is flagged **[unverified]**.

---

## 1. What the platform actually does

**Core entity:** The National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (NCRP), run by the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) under the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA), launched in its current form around 2019-2020 with a major relaunch/upgrade cited as December 11, 2023 ([Wikipedia — National Cybercrime Reporting Portal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cybercrime_Reporting_Portal)).

**Core user flows:**
- **File a complaint** at cybercrime.gov.in — two tracks: (a) "Report Women/Child Related Crime" (can be filed anonymously), (b) "Report Other Cyber Crimes," which covers financial fraud, hacking, identity theft etc. Filing generates a 14- or 16-digit acknowledgment number ([righttoinformation.wiki, 2026](https://righttoinformation.wiki/check-status/cybercrime-complaint-status)).
- **1930 helpline** — the national 24x7 cyber-fraud-specific number, distinct from the general portal, meant for *financial* fraud where speed matters. It received **3.24 crore calls in 2025** ([search summary citing 2025 data](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/)).
- **Fund freeze / CFCFRMS** — the Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting and Management System sits behind 1930 and the portal's financial-fraud flow. When a victim reports fast enough, I4C/CFCFRMS pushes an alert into the banking/UPI/wallet network to place a "lien" (hold) on the money before it is withdrawn or layered into further accounts. This is explicitly a "golden hour" mechanism — the first ~60 minutes after fraud is when freezing is realistically possible ([Deccan Herald, Karnataka golden-hour piece](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/karnataka-mulls-golden-hour-intervention-to-get-the-better-of-cybercriminals-903939.html)).
- **Complaint status tracking** — "Track Your Complaint" on the portal using the acknowledgment ID. Status states are roughly Submitted → Assigned to Cyber Cell → Under Investigation → Resolved/Closed/Disposed ([mywestbengal.com, National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal guide](https://mywestbengal.com/national-cyber-crime-reporting/); [righttoinformation.wiki](https://righttoinformation.wiki/check-status/cybercrime-complaint-status)).
- **Escalation path if stuck:** call 1930 for an update, visit the local cyber cell with the complaint ID, file an RTI under §6 (30-day mandatory reply) if the SLA is breached, or — if police simply do not act — file a petition before a Judicial Magistrate under BNSS §175 ([vidhikarya.com](https://www.vidhikarya.com/legal-blog/after-filing-cybercrime-complaint-india); [righttoinformation.wiki](https://righttoinformation.wiki/check-status/cybercrime-complaint-status)).
- **Jurisdiction rule:** any local police station is legally bound to register a Zero FIR regardless of jurisdiction and transfer it later; cybercrime jurisdiction itself follows BNSS §178 (formerly CrPC §177) — filed where the crime occurred, where the accused is found, or where the effect of the crime is felt ([vidhikarya.com](https://www.vidhikarya.com/legal-blog/after-filing-cybercrime-complaint-india)).

## 2. Where it concretely fails users today

### A. Money reported ≠ money recovered — the gap is enormous
- From **April 2021 to November 2025**, CFCFRMS stopped roughly **₹7,647 crore** from reaching fraudsters out of about **₹52,969 crore reported**, but only about **₹167 crore was actually restored to victims — a 2.18% restoration rate** ([search-aggregated figure from 2025/2026 coverage]). **[Note: this specific figure combination came back from a single aggregated search result and could not be independently cross-verified against a primary MHA document in this pass — flag as needs-verification before quoting in a pitch deck, though the order of magnitude is consistent with other cited numbers below.]**
- More recent (and better-sourced) parliamentary figures: as of **31 December 2025**, MHA told Rajya Sabha that **more than ₹8,189 crore has been saved across 23.61 lakh complaints** since 2021 via CFCFRMS ([ETV Bharat, "Rs 8,189 Crore Saved From Cyber Criminals Between 2021 And 2025: Govt In Rajya Sabha"](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/several-crore-saved-from-cyber-criminals-between-2021-and-2025-government-in-rajya-sabha-enn26020405651)); a related figure of **₹7,130 crore saved in 23.02 lakh complaints** was given to Lok Sabha in a December 2025 answer to MP Nathwani ([DeshGujarat, 3 Dec 2025](https://deshgujarat.com/2025/12/03/over-rs-7130-crore-saved-in-23-02-lakh-cyber-fraud-complaints-govt-to-rs-mp-nathwani/)); a March 2026 statement to the Lok Sabha by MoS Home B. Sanjay Kumar cites **₹8,600 crore saved** ([News on Air, 11 Mar 2026](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/govt-saves-over-%e2%82%b98600-crore-in-cyber-fraud-cases-mos-home-affairs-b-sanjay-kumar)).
- Even taking the government's own best-case "saved" figure (money frozen/held, not necessarily returned) against total reported losses: **2025 alone saw ₹22,495 crore lost across 28.15 lakh (2.81 million) complaints, a 24% year-on-year rise** ([search-aggregated, multiple 2025/2026 sources citing NCRP data]). I4C itself has estimated cyber fraud losses could **exceed ₹1.2 lakh crore in 2025** on a broader definition ([search-aggregated]).
- "Saved"/"frozen" is not "returned to the victim." Multiple legal-service explainers note that even after a successful freeze, actually getting the money back into the victim's account requires a separate, often slow, court/bank-NOC process — the portal has no visible end-to-end status for this second step ([vidhikarya.com, "How can victims recover money from cyber fraud in India"](https://www.vidhikarya.com/legal-blog/how-to-recover-money-cyber-fraud-india)).
- Localized data point: Mumbai Police froze **₹101.39 crore between January and May 2025**, a reported **~25.68% success/freeze rate** against total fraud reported to them ([Newkerala, "Mumbai Police Freeze Rs 101 Crore in Cyber Fraud Funds"](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/mumbai-cyber-police-freeze-rs-101-crore-fraud-836.htm)); another regional data point shows lien/freeze rate rising from **13.40% (Jan-Apr 2025) to 21.23% (Jan-Apr 2026)**, with overall "recovery efficiency" cited at **22.22%** for that state ([The Goan EveryDay, "Cyber fraud recovery improves, funds' loss declines: Official data"](https://www.thegoan.net/goa-news/%EF%BB%BFcyber-fraud-recovery-improves-funds-loss-declines-official-data/148737.html)). Even the *best*-performing jurisdictions cited are recovering roughly one rupee in four or five — most fraud money is simply gone.

### B. "Disposed" / "Closed" status is opaque and often means nothing happened
- The status label "closed" or "disposed" can mean the case was transferred to a different jurisdiction, that the preliminary inquiry found insufficient grounds, or that it was converted into a formal FIR elsewhere — the portal gives the victim no plain-language explanation of which of these applies ([vidhikarya.com](https://www.vidhikarya.com/legal-blog/after-filing-cybercrime-complaint-india); explainer specifically titled ["Cybercrime Complaint Closed But Portal Shows Pending?"](https://sudhirrao.com/cybercrime-complaint-closed-but-portal-shows-pending-what-it-means/) and ["Cybercrime Complaint Shows Disposed but Nothing Happened? Reopen It"](https://righttoinformation.wiki/practical-guides/cybercrime-portal-complaint-disposed-without-action)). The existence of multiple 2025-2026-dated explainer articles specifically about "disposed but nothing happened" is itself evidence this is a common, recognized user complaint.
- Victims' only real recourse when a case sits idle past its SLA is procedural and slow: RTI (30-day statutory reply) or a magistrate petition under BNSS §175 — neither is designed for a fraud victim trying to get money back within days, and neither is exposed anywhere in the portal's UI ([righttoinformation.wiki](https://righttoinformation.wiki/check-status/cybercrime-complaint-status)).

### C. Innocent people get their accounts frozen as collateral damage, with no fast way out
- When money passes through a chain of accounts (fraud money is typically routed through **5-6 intermediate accounts** before reaching the fraudster), banks/police often freeze *every* account in the chain, including accounts belonging to people who received money for a legitimate reason and had no idea it was tainted ([news4hackers.com, "Fraud Prevention Efforts' Unintended Consequences Affect Innocent Bank Account Holders"](https://www.news4hackers.com/fraud-prevention-efforts-unintended-consequences-affect-innocent-bank-account-holders)).
- Concrete case: Hyderabad software engineer Pradeep Sharma received ₹18,000 for a second-hand laptop sold on OLX; **three weeks later** his account was frozen with no direct notice explaining why ([news4hackers.com](https://www.news4hackers.com/fraud-prevention-efforts-unintended-consequences-affect-innocent-bank-account-holders)).
- Scale of the freeze dragnet: the CBI identified **nearly 8.5 lakh mule accounts** opened across 700 bank branches in 2025 ([The420.in, "Cybercrime Networks Find New Ground: Banks Freeze 8.5 Lakh Mule Accounts"](https://the420.in/mule-accounts-freeze-cybercrime-shift-rbi-i4c-fraud-networks/)); between September 2024 and January 2026, I4C shared details of **more than 2.73 million suspected "Layer 1" mule accounts** with banks, blocking transactions worth over **₹9,518 crore** ([search-aggregated, same period]). At that volume, some proportion of frozen accounts are, by the "5-6 hop" logic above, near-certainly innocent third parties — and there is a dedicated 420.in investigative piece specifically titled **"Fraud Crackdown's Hidden Cost: Innocent Account Holders Face Freezes As Mule Networks Grow"** ([The420.in](https://the420.in/cyber-fraud-crackdown-account-freeze-mule-network-cost/)).
- The unfreeze process for an innocent account holder is manual and adversarial by design: approach the cyber cell, *prove you are the victim and not the accused*, obtain a No Objection Certificate (NOC), and submit it to the bank — with no defined SLA and no portal-visible status for this specific sub-process ([LegalFund, "How Cyber Crime Complaints Freeze Bank Accounts in India — And How to Unfreeze Them (2026)"](https://legalfund.in/how-cyber-crime-complaints-freeze-bank-accounts-in-india/); [globalvisionlawfirm.com](https://globalvisionlawfirm.com/bank-account-frozen-due-to-cyber-fraud-complaint-legal-steps-to-unfreeze-it-in-india/)).
- Bankers themselves want more power here, not less: the Indian Banks' Association (IBA) has been **pushing RBI for explicit statutory power to freeze mule accounts** faster, which implies the current legal basis for freezing (and, symmetrically, for releasing wrongly-frozen accounts) is itself ambiguous ([Medianama, "IBA Pushes for RBI Power to Freeze Mule Bank Accounts," April 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/04/223-iba-rbi-cyber-fraud-measures-freeze-bank-accounts-cybercrime/)).

### D. Time is the enemy, and most victims don't move fast enough — nor does the system
- The entire freeze mechanism only works within the "golden hour" — roughly the first 60 minutes after a fraudulent transaction, before money is layered across multiple accounts and cashed out or converted to crypto ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/karnataka-mulls-karnataka-mulls-golden-hour-intervention-to-get-the-better-of-cybercriminals-903939.html)). Yet most people do not know the 1930 number exists, do not know that a general FIR is slower than the 1930/CFCFRMS route, and the standard advice literature explicitly frames "acting within the golden hour" as something the *victim* has to already know to do — the system offers no proactive nudge once a suspicious transaction is flagged elsewhere (e.g. by the bank itself).

### E. Confusing categorization at the point of filing
- Multiple third-party explainer sites exist purely to help users pick the correct complaint category / determine whether their case is "Women/Child" vs "Other Cyber Crimes" vs an outright financial-fraud fast-track — a strong signal that the portal's own category taxonomy is not self-explanatory to a stressed, non-technical victim ([righttoinformation.wiki, "How to file a cybercrime complaint — complete 2026 guide"](https://righttoinformation.wiki/file-cybercrime-complaint-2026); [prashantkanha.com, "Cyber Fraud Crime: Complaints and Redressal in 2026"](https://www.prashantkanha.com/ncrp-cfcfrms-cyber-fraud-sop-2026/)).

## 3. Root causes — backend/process, not UI

This maps cleanly onto the hackathon's "fix the backend, not the screen" requirement. The pain is structurally *not* about button placement or form design:

1. **No unified status object across agencies.** A single fraud case touches at minimum: the portal/I4C intake, the local police station (which may Zero-FIR and hand off), the destination bank(s) across possibly 5-6 hops, NPCI/UPI rails, and sometimes a different state's cyber cell entirely. Each of these systems has its own internal status, and none of them is synthesized into one victim-facing timeline. "Disposed" on the portal can be true from I4C's point of view while the money-recovery process is still ongoing at a bank three hops away — the victim has no way to know which.
2. **Freeze and release are asymmetric processes.** Freezing an account is fast and centralized (I4C pushes an alert, banks act on it). *Releasing* a wrongly-frozen or partially-cleared account is slow, manual, and requires the account holder to personally chase a cyber cell for an NOC — there is no reciprocal automated "clear this account" signal once an investigation rules someone out. This is a genuine backend/process gap, not a UI gap.
3. **Jurisdiction handoff has no tracked SLA.** BNSS mandates a Zero FIR + transfer, but nothing in the public-facing system tells a victim *when* that transfer happened, to whom, or what the new SLA clock is — this is a process design gap (no state machine exposed to the citizen), not a screen redesign problem.
4. **"Golden hour" dependency with no systemic trigger.** The system's only real point of leverage (freezing before layering) depends on the victim personally acting within ~60 minutes and knowing to call 1930 specifically rather than filing a general complaint — there is no backend hook where a bank's own fraud-detection signal auto-triggers an I4C freeze-request without the victim being the one to initiate it.

## 4. What's already been tried

- **1930 helpline + cloud upgrade.** An upgraded cloud-based 1930 call centre with I4C-deployed personnel now freezes suspicious transactions across the banking network with fewer procedural steps than before ([search-aggregated 2025/2026 coverage of the 1930 relaunch]). It received 3.24 crore calls in 2025.
- **CFCFRMS** — the structured financial-fraud reporting/lien system sitting behind 1930, credited with saving ₹7,130-8,690 crore (varying parliamentary answers, Dec 2025-Mar 2026) since 2021 across roughly 23-24 lakh complaints.
- **I4C mule-account data sharing with banks/RBI** — over 2.73 million Layer-1 mule accounts shared Sept 2024-Jan 2026, blocking >₹9,518 crore in transactions; RBI expanding real-time data integration with NPCI and NCRP ([The420.in](https://the420.in/mule-accounts-freeze-cybercrime-shift-rbi-i4c-fraud-networks/)).
- **Fraud Risk Indicator (FRI)** — a newer joint mechanism (with Ministry of Communications / telecom data) credited with preventing over ₹660 crore in cyber fraud losses in 6 months as of Dec 2025 ([News on Air, 22 Dec 2025](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/government-prevents-%e2%82%b9660-crore-cyber-fraud-losses-in-6-months-using-fraud-risk-indicator)).
- **IBA lobbying RBI for explicit statutory freeze powers** (April 2025) — signals banks themselves think the legal/process plumbing for freezing (and by extension, un-freezing) is still inadequate.

**Why gaps remain despite all this:** every initiative listed above targets the *front end of the pipeline* — reporting faster, freezing faster, sharing mule-account lists faster. None of them targets the *citizen-facing transparency and reconciliation layer* — what happened to my specific case, is my account correctly or wrongly frozen, and when do I get my money or my account back. That is the open backend/process gap the hackathon brief rewards.

## 5. Buildathon fit assessment

- **Fully mockable, no live-system risk.** A synthetic dataset of complaint records (acknowledgment ID, category, amount, timestamp, jurisdiction) plus a synthetic multi-hop fund-freeze timeline (bank A→B→C, freeze status per hop, NOC status) can be built entirely from public knowledge of the process without touching cybercrime.gov.in, any bank API, or any real PII. No real Aadhaar/PAN/OTP/payment data is needed — this is one of the safer platforms in the list precisely because the *whole point* is synthesizing status across systems that a hackathon team cannot access live anyway.
- **Mock dataset needed:** (a) a synthetic complaint table (category, amount, filing time, "golden hour" countdown, jurisdiction/police station assigned, FIR/Zero-FIR status), (b) a synthetic fund-hop table (list of intermediate accounts, per-hop freeze/lien status, per-hop "cleared / still under investigation / wrongly frozen" flag, NOC issuance status), and (c) a synthetic bank-side account-holder record for the "am I an innocent hop-through victim" flow.
- **3-day feasibility:** high. The reasoning layer (translate scattered per-agency statuses into one plain-language timeline + next action) is the valuable, buildable part; it does not require real-time integration to demo convincingly.
- **Risk/sensitivity note:** must clearly label everything synthetic and avoid implying it connects to real bank rails — same caution the brief already requires generally.

## 6. Candidate "fix the failing moment" problem statements

> **"My status says Disposed. Nobody told me what that means."** A victim files a fraud complaint, watches the acknowledgment number generate, and weeks later sees the status flip to "Disposed" or "Closed" with zero explanation of which of the several possible meanings applies — insufficient evidence, transferred to another jurisdiction, or converted to a formal FIR elsewhere ([vidhikarya.com](https://www.vidhikarya.com/legal-blog/after-filing-cybercrime-complaint-india); [sudhirrao.com, "Cybercrime Complaint Closed But Portal Shows Pending?"](https://sudhirrao.com/cybercrime-complaint-closed-but-portal-shows-pending-what-it-means/); [righttoinformation.wiki, "Cybercrime Complaint Shows Disposed but Nothing Happened? Reopen It"](https://righttoinformation.wiki/practical-guides/cybercrime-portal-complaint-disposed-without-action)). The proliferation of 2025-2026-dated explainer articles that exist purely to decode this one status field is itself the evidence: real victims are googling "what does disposed mean" instead of getting an answer from the system. Strong buildathon fit: a mock complaint-lifecycle dataset (status codes mapped to plain-language meaning + the specific next action for each: RTI template, magistrate petition template, or "wait, this is normal") is fully synthetic, and the valuable part is the translation/decision layer, not any live case data.

> **"The fraud money is gone, but so is my own account — and nobody will tell me why."** Fraud money typically routes through 5-6 intermediate bank accounts before reaching the fraudster, and current practice is to freeze every account in that chain — including people who received the money for an unrelated, legitimate reason. Hyderabad engineer Pradeep Sharma had his account frozen three weeks after receiving ₹18,000 for an OLX laptop sale, with no proactive notice of why ([news4hackers.com](https://www.news4hackers.com/fraud-prevention-efforts-unintended-consequences-affect-innocent-bank-account-holders)); the CBI identified nearly 8.5 lakh mule accounts across 700 branches in 2025, and I4C shared 2.73 million suspected mule accounts with banks between Sept 2024 and Jan 2026 ([The420.in](https://the420.in/mule-accounts-freeze-cybercrime-shift-rbi-i4c-fraud-networks/)) — a scale at which innocent hop-through freezes are a structural certainty, not an edge case, and there is a dedicated investigative piece on exactly this cost ([The420.in, "Fraud Crackdown's Hidden Cost"](https://the420.in/cyber-fraud-crackdown-account-freeze-mule-network-cost/)). Today's only fix is manual: go to the cyber cell, prove you're a victim not a suspect, get an NOC, hand it to the bank — no SLA, no visible status. Strong buildathon fit: mock a multi-hop transaction chain, mark one hop as "innocent recipient," and build the process/timeline that should exist — an automatic notification the moment a freeze is applied, a self-service evidence-upload flow, and an SLA-tracked release status — instead of the current silent, adversarial process.

> **"Did my case actually get transferred, or is it just stuck?"** Local police stations are legally required to register a Zero FIR regardless of jurisdiction and transfer it onward under BNSS §178, but the portal gives no visible confirmation of when — or whether — that handoff happened ([vidhikarya.com](https://www.vidhikarya.com/legal-blog/after-filing-cybercrime-complaint-india)). A victim's only recourse if the case appears to have stalled is an RTI (30-day statutory reply) or a magistrate petition under BNSS §175 ([righttoinformation.wiki](https://righttoinformation.wiki/check-status/cybercrime-complaint-status)) — both are processes designed for grievance redress in general, not for a fraud victim trying to track a case that may already be sitting, untouched, at a second police station. Strong buildathon fit: mock the jurisdiction-transfer state machine (filed → Zero FIR registered → transferred → received by destination station → SLA clock reset) as a visible timeline with automatic escalation triggers when a stage overstays its SLA — this is a pure backend/process fix, since the underlying legal mechanism (BNSS §178) already exists and just isn't exposed to the citizen.

> **"I called within the golden hour — did the freeze actually happen, and where's my money now?"** The entire fund-recovery mechanism depends on freezing within roughly the first 60 minutes before money is layered across accounts ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/karnataka-mulls-golden-hour-intervention-to-get-the-better-of-cybercriminals-903939.html)), and government figures show freeze/lien rates even in the best-performing jurisdictions sit around 21-26% of reported amounts ([The Goan EveryDay](https://www.thegoan.net/goa-news/%EF%BB%BFcyber-fraud-recovery-improves-funds-loss-declines-official-data/148737.html); [Newkerala, Mumbai Police data](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/mumbai-cyber-police-freeze-rs-101-crore-fraud-836.htm)) — meaning most victims who did everything right still don't know, in the following days, whether their specific rupees were frozen, are still moving, or are already gone. Even a successful freeze does not equal a returned rupee: the freeze-to-restoration step is a separate, opaque, and often much slower court/bank process ([vidhikarya.com](https://www.vidhikarya.com/legal-blog/how-to-recover-money-cyber-fraud-india)). **Update, 2026-08-26:** MHA/I4C launched the Money Restoration Module (`mrm-ncrp.mha.gov.in`) around 20 Aug 2026, giving the original complainant a tracked path (request ID, then a Unique Request Number) to reclaim funds identified for restoration. This closes the tracking gap for the person who filed the NCRP complaint. It does not touch the portal's uninvolved-recipient problem below, since MRM's own login requires the complainant's NCRP acknowledgement number, something the bystander this research is about never has. Good buildathon fit: mock a single victim's fund trail hop by hop (frozen / still moving / released to fraudster) with live-feeling status updates, showing the reasoning layer that turns "your money is somewhere in the CFCFRMS pipeline" into a plain, specific answer — this is squarely a backend transparency fix, not a redesign of the reporting form.

> **"Which complaint category do I even pick, and does it change what happens next?"** The portal splits intake into "Women/Child Related Crime" (anonymous-eligible) versus "Other Cyber Crimes," inside which financial fraud has its own fast-track via 1930/CFCFRMS — a taxonomy opaque enough that multiple independent 2026-dated guide sites exist solely to help users choose correctly before filing ([righttoinformation.wiki, "How to file a cybercrime complaint — complete 2026 guide"](https://righttoinformation.wiki/file-cybercrime-complaint-2026); [prashantkanha.com](https://www.prashantkanha.com/ncrp-cfcfrms-cyber-fraud-sop-2026/)). A wrong category choice at the point of filing risks losing the "golden hour" freeze window entirely if a financial-fraud victim files through the slower general track instead of 1930. Moderate buildathon fit: this is closer to UI/routing than the other four, but the underlying fix — auto-detecting "this looks like financial fraud in progress, route to CFCFRMS fast-track now" — is a backend triage/routing decision, not a cosmetic form redesign, so it can be framed to satisfy the brief if paired with one of the timeline/status ideas above rather than built alone.

---

## Summary of strongest fit

The **innocent-mule-account-freeze** idea and the **"disposed means what?" status-decoder** idea are the strongest candidates: both are backed by hard numbers (8.5 lakh mule accounts, 2.73 million shared with banks, ₹9,518 crore blocked), both have a documented, named victim story to demo against, both are entirely mockable with synthetic multi-hop account data, and both fix a genuine backend/process gap (asymmetric freeze-vs-release, and no unified cross-agency status) rather than a screen.

## 7. Live walkthrough findings (2026-08-25) — observed on cybercrime.gov.in, nothing submitted

Added after a browser walkthrough of the live portal. No form was submitted, no OTP requested, no personal data entered.

- **Three tiles, three different identity rules, none explained.** Women/Children Related Crime offers "Report Anonymously" *and* "Register & Track"; Financial Fraud and Other Cyber Crime offer only "Register a Complaint" and both go to `Crime_AuthoLogin.aspx?rnt=5` (mobile + OTP + captcha). Anonymity is scoped by the FAQ to RGR content only. A Women/Child complaint **can never be withdrawn**; a Report Cyber Crime complaint can be withdrawn only *before FIR conversion*.
- **Two consent interstitials on the same URL** (`/Webform/Accept.aspx`) before any field appears. The first carries 112 / 181 / **1930** — the only place on that path the 1930 route is mentioned.
- **The "Check list for complainant" appears only at the login wall**, after both consent screens. Verbatim: incident date/time; incident details **minimum 200 characters**, no special characters; national ID soft copy in .jpeg/.jpg/.png **≤5 MB**; for financial fraud — bank/wallet/merchant name, **12-digit transaction id/UTR**, transaction date, fraud amount; evidence **"not more than 10 MB each"**. This **contradicts** the citizen manual's "maximum file size of 5 mb".
- **Complaint form structure, observed via the anonymous door** (no login needed): 3-step wizard — Complaint & Incident Details / Suspect Details / Preview & Submit. Step 2 is **inert until step 1 validates**, so the citizen cannot see what will be asked next. Step 1 fields: Category (4 options on the anonymous path); date + separate HH/MM/AM-PM dropdowns; "Reason for delay in reporting" (free text, not required); State → District → Police Station cascade; **"Where did the incident occur?"** offering only Email, Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, Twitter, WhatsApp, Website Url, Youtube, LinkedIn, Telegram, Other — **no phone call, SMS, in-person or bank-app option**; free text **min 200 / max 1500 chars**, "Special Characters like ~!#^'`$|{}<>* are not allowed".
- **Tracking requires the filing phone.** `/Webform/chkackstatus.aspx` = Acknowledgement No. + **OTP to the registered mobile** + captcha. The manual's in-portal route is username + mobile + OTP + security answer, then a **date-range search**, not a lookup by case ID.
- **No official enumeration of NCRP status values exists** — not in the manual, FAQ, or any I4C document reachable. Third-party guides disagree with each other. **This is the single most important unknown for a status-decoder build.** The Money Restoration Module (added 2026-08-26, see above) introduces its own tracked states, request ID, then URN, then an eligible-amount SMS, but only for the original complainant's refund path, not for a general NCRP status vocabulary.
- **The 24-hour rule, verbatim**, from `instructions_citizenreportingcyberfrauds.pdf` (still linked live, titled *"For Delhi Only"*): *"After reporting of complaint/incident, the complainant will get a system generated Log-in Id/acknowledgement number through SMS/Mail. Using the above Log-in Id/acknowledgement number, the complainant must complete registration of complaint on National Cybercrime Reporting Portal (www.cybercrime.gov.in) within 24 hours. This is mandatory."* A 1930 call that is not converted on the portal within 24 hours is not a complaint. **Verify this is still enforced nationally before using it in a pitch.**
- **1930 captures exactly seven fields**: complainant mobile; bank/wallet/merchant name; account/wallet/merchant/UPI ID debited; transaction ID; transaction date; card number if card credentials used; screenshot if available.
- **Escalation is weak by design.** Contact Us lists State/UT Nodal Cyber Cell and Grievance Officers, but only for "Report & Track" complainants — anonymous filers get nothing. **Every email is obfuscated** (`[at]`, `[dot]`, `[hyphen]`) and must be reassembled by hand. **No SLA is stated anywhere.**
- **Report Suspect takes no login** (`/Webform/cyber_suspect.aspx`): state + type (Website URL, WhatsApp/Telegram, phone, email, SMS header, social media URL, **Deepfake**, **Mobile App**) + one file ≤5 MB + 500-char description. The **public Suspect Repository** is searchable by mobile/email/bank account/UPI/social handle and I4C disclaims its accuracy; the only remedy if wrongly listed is an email to `cyberdost[at]mha[dot]gov[dot]in`. Same freeze/release asymmetry as the bank-account problem.
- **Official manual is stale.** `MHA-CitizenManualReportOtherCyberCrime-v10.pdf` is dated **30 Aug 2019** — older than the Financial Fraud tile and the Suspect Repository now live on the site.
- **Evidence file types (Annexure B, verbatim):** `.txt .png .jpeg .jiff .rtf .jpg .dib .gif .doc .ppt .docx .pptx .pdf .epub .bmp .avi .wmv .3gp .mp4 .mkv .mov .flv .mpg .webm`, max 5 MB, added one at a time. **No documented cap on file count.** `.wav`/`.amr` are referenced elsewhere in the portal's own material but are **not** on the accepted list — a voice recording of a vishing call has no documented way in. Uploaded evidence gets a **hash value** for integrity.
- **Volume/loss, MHA figures via parliamentary answers:** NCRP incidents 4.52 lakh (2021) → 10.29 (2022) → 15.96 (2023) → 22.68 lakh (2024); reported loss ₹2,290 cr (2022) → ₹7,465 cr (2023) → ₹22,846 cr (2024). CFCFRMS: **₹11,158 crore saved across 32.80 lakh complaints as on 30 June 2026** (PIB/I4C).
- **No published FIR-conversion rate, by design.** MHA's standing line: conversion into FIRs "and subsequent action are handled by the State/UT Law Enforcement Agencies concerned." **e-Zero FIR** (May 2025) auto-generates a Zero FIR at the e-Crime Police Station, Delhi, only for losses **above ₹10 lakh**.
- **Blocked to automation:** mha.gov.in returns HTTP 403. Parliamentary figures above reached us via secondary quotation of LS 22.07.2025 Q.344, LS 02.12.2025 Q.432/Q.452, RS 27.11.2024 Q.226 — open by hand before quoting.
