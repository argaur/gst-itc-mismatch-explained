# Research: GST Portal (gst.gov.in) — Build What Moves India

Pulled 2026-08-24. Sources cited inline; anything not independently verifiable is flagged **[unverified]**.

---

## 1. What the platform actually does

The GST Portal (run by GSTN, Goods and Services Tax Network) is the single system of record for India's indirect tax. Core flows a small business or individual touches:

- **GSTIN registration (REG-01 onward):** new businesses apply with PAN, business address proof, bank details, and (for many categories) Aadhaar authentication. A jurisdictional officer can approve, seek clarification, or reject. As of 2025, most straightforward applications are meant to clear via Aadhaar-authenticated auto-approval; "risky"/flagged applicants go to manual physical verification.
- **Return filing cycle**, monthly or quarterly:
  - **GSTR-1** — outward supply (sales) details, invoice-wise.
  - **GSTR-2A** — auto-populated, dynamic, near-real-time view of what suppliers have uploaded against the buyer's GSTIN.
  - **GSTR-2B** — a static, month-locked ITC statement generated on a fixed date, meant to be the authoritative basis for claiming input tax credit (ITC) in GSTR-3B.
  - **GSTR-3B** — the summary return where tax is actually paid and ITC is claimed/offset.
- **ITC reconciliation:** the taxpayer (or their software) must match purchase-side records against GSTR-2A/2B to make sure they only claim ITC that suppliers have actually reported, since GSTR-3B is self-declared but downstream-verified.
- **E-way bills:** a document required to move goods above a value threshold, generated on a separate portal (ewaybillgst.gov.in) but tightly gated by GST return-filing status — if returns are overdue, e-way bill generation can be blocked.
- **Refunds:** exporters and inverted-duty-structure businesses file refund applications through the portal for excess ITC/tax paid.
- **Notices:** the department (increasingly a rules engine, not a human officer) issues notices — DRC-01B (liability mismatch, GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B), DRC-01C (ITC mismatch, GSTR-3B vs GSTR-2B), and formal show-cause notices under Sections 73/74 — that the taxpayer must respond to within a fixed window or face a demand order, interest, and penalty.

## 2. Where it concretely fails users today

**ITC mismatch is the dominant pain point.** ITC mismatch arises "when there is a discrepancy between the ITC claimed by a taxpayer in GSTR-3B and the ITC reflected in GSTR-2A/2B," typically because a supplier delayed filing, mis-keyed a GSTIN or invoice number, or filed in a different period than the buyer's claim ([IRIS MSME](https://irismsme.com/blog/gst-reconciliation-msmes-claim-missing-itc/); [AuthBridge](https://authbridge.com/knowledge-centre/insights/correct-itc-mismatch-gst)). Critically, the buyer's ITC is contingent on a *supplier's* filing behaviour they don't control — a small MSME buyer can do everything right and still lose credit because a vendor upstream missed a deadline ([TaxTMI on MSME suppliers/45-day payment compliance](https://www.taxtmi.com/forum/issue?id=120913)).

**Notices are now algorithmic, not human-reviewed, and land on ordinary businesses.** From FY 2024-25, the GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B and GSTR-3B vs GSTR-2B comparisons "run automatically for every GSTIN after each GSTR-3B submission," triggering DRC-01B and DRC-01C notices without officer involvement when deviations cross a threshold ([Cygnet](https://www.cygnet.one/blog/drc-01b-and-drc-01c-gst-notices/); [Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/learn/drc-01b-gst-liability-mismatch-guide/)). GST notices "use legal and technical language" that ordinary traders can't parse, and mixing up response forms (e.g., confusing DRC-03 with DRC-06) "can cost a client significantly" ([SSCOIndia](https://www.sscoindia.com/blog/how-to-reply-gst-drc-01-notice-india-guide-sscoindia); [Tax Garden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/gst-drc-01-show-cause-notice-reply-drc-06-guide-india-2026)). India Law Offices describes a documented "surge of notices" tied to ITC mismatches ([indialawoffices.com](https://www.indialawoffices.com/legal-articles/gst-taxpayers-jeopardy-surge-notices-mismatch-itc)).

**The portal goes down at the worst possible time, repeatedly.** In January 2025, the portal was inaccessible in the run-up to the GSTR-1 deadline: as of 4 PM IST on January 10, 2025 (deadline January 11), the site remained down. GSTN itself acknowledged the outage publicly, filed an incident report with CBIC, and requested a filing-date extension ([Vygr News](https://vygrnews.com/business/is-the-gst-portal-down-causing-major-disruptions-ahead-of-the-gstr-1-deadline-); [Gujarat Samachar](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/national/gst-portal-down-just-hours-before-the-deadline-businesspersons-ask-for-extensions-87101943887.html)). This is not a one-off — CAs and trade bodies have repeatedly petitioned for deadline extensions citing portal instability near due dates ([A2Z Taxcorp](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/extend-gstr-3b-return-filing-deadline-demand-by-advocates-and-cas-as-the-portal-is-not-working-properly/); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/gstn-seeks-extension-of-gstr-1-filing-deadline-as-technical-snag-hits-system-3349756)).

**Registration delays and rejections were bad enough that CBIC had to intervene.** CBIC's own Instruction No. 03/2025-GST (17 April 2025) instructs officers to stop demanding irrelevant documents and to speed up Aadhaar-authenticated approvals — an implicit admission that officers were routinely over-demanding paperwork and slow-walking approvals ([SSCOIndia](https://www.sscoindia.com/blog/how-to-avoid-gst-registration-rejection-2025-cbics-new-guidelines)). Before the fix, "many small businesses struggled with long GST registration timelines, often waiting 7–10 days or more" ([MyGSTRefund](https://www.mygstrefund.com/news/gst-3-day-approval)). The most frequent rejection trigger cited for 2025-26 is a document-matching problem, not a substantive one: "mismatch between the registered office address in REG-01 and the supporting document — electricity bill, rent agreement, or property tax receipt," and shared premises without a proper NOC ([AABK](https://www.aabk.in/blog-details/common-reasons-for-gst-registration-rejection-and-how-to-fix-them)). In response, the GST Council approved a simplified registration scheme (rolled out November 1, 2025) for low-risk small businesses self-assessing under Rs 2.5 lakh/month output tax liability, with Rule 9A/14A promising 3-working-day approval where before it took a week-plus ([MyGSTRefund](https://www.mygstrefund.com/news/gst-3-day-approval)).

**Refunds sit blocked, tying up MSME working capital.** Deloitte's GST@9 survey found MSMEs explicitly asking for faster refunds and better working capital support, and separately reported that 89% of respondents want automatic interest payment on delayed refunds, 88% want invoice-based ITC eligibility, and 87% back quarterly payment mechanisms ([Deloitte press release](https://www.deloitte.com/in/en/about/press-room/msmes-back-gst-reforms-call-for-faster-refunds-and-better-working-capital-support-deloitte-India-gst-9-survey.html); [CXOToday](https://cxotoday.com/governance/msmes-applaud-gst-reforms-but-urge-quicker-refunds-capital-support/)). Government data on Central Tax refund pendency shows the backlog itself is not the whole story — claims over 90 days fell to 110 claims worth ₹64.10 crore by end FY2025-26 — but interest paid on delayed refunds totalled ₹16.60 crore across FY2023-24 to FY2025-26, meaning delay is still routine enough to generate a running interest liability ([TaxGuru citing government data](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/government-shares-gst-refund-pendency-delay-measures-exporters-msmes.html)). Gujarat MSMEs specifically flagged delayed refunds and inverted duty structure as live pain points ([A2Z Taxcorp](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/gujarat-msmes-raise-issue-of-delayed-refunds-inverted-duty-structure-in-gst/)).

**Suo moto (automatic) GSTIN cancellation is a cliff-edge failure mode for small businesses.** A GSTIN gets cancelled unilaterally by the officer if returns aren't filed for a continuous period (commonly cited: 6 months for regular filers), most often driven by "lack of awareness or cash flow constraints" rather than fraud ([Tax2win](https://tax2win.in/guide/suo-moto-cancellation-gst-registration); [GimBooks](https://www.gimbooks.com/blog/suo-moto-cancellation-of-gst-registration/)). After cancellation, the taxpayer "would not be allowed to file return or upload invoices" for the cancelled period, cutting off their own ability to fix the underlying miss — a genuine catch-22 that can only be escaped by filing everything due and going through revocation ([TaxBuddy](https://www.taxbuddy.com/blog/suo-moto-cancellation-gst-registration)).

**E-way bills fail at the exact moment of dispatch.** A 180-day rule (effective 1 Jan 2025) blocks e-way bill generation for any invoice dated more than 180 days prior — goods can be packed and ready with no way to move them legally ([FilewithUs](https://filewithus.ai/blog/e-way-bill-generation-guide)). E-way bill generation is also gated on GST return-filing status, so a small transporter or trader with a lapsed filing can be blocked from moving goods altogether, and common errors (wrong GSTIN, vehicle number, late generation after departure) trigger detention and penalties that MSMEs are disproportionately exposed to because they lack dedicated compliance staff ([GimBooks](https://www.gimbooks.com/blog/avoid-penalties-common-errors-in-e-way-bill-generation-and-their-solutions/)).

## 3. Root causes: backend/process, not UI

This maps cleanly onto backend/process failure, which is what the hackathon wants:

- **ITC matching logic is fundamentally third-party-dependent.** The buyer's ITC eligibility is computed from the *supplier's* filing behaviour (GSTR-2A/2B), not the buyer's own transaction record. This is a data-pipeline/dependency design choice, not a screen problem — no amount of UI polish fixes a credit denial caused by someone else's late filing.
- **Notice generation has moved to a rules engine with a fixed comparison logic (GSTR-1 vs 3B, 3B vs 2B) and a hard response clock, but zero plain-language translation layer.** The backend decision ("liability mismatch > threshold → issue DRC-01B") is sound tax administration; the failure is that the system hands the citizen a legal document with no explanation of *why* the mismatch happened, *which specific transaction* caused it, or *what the safe response is*. That's a missing reasoning/explanation layer sitting on top of an otherwise correct backend signal — exactly the kind of gap a hackathon prototype can fill without touching the real compliance engine.
- **Registration verification pipeline over-triggers manual review and document mismatches**, which is why CBIC had to issue a corrective instruction and the Council had to legislate a new fast lane (Rule 9A/14A) rather than just relabel a screen.
- **Portal downtime near deadlines is a capacity/load-engineering problem** (system architecture, not front-end), repeatedly acknowledged by GSTN itself.
- **Suo moto cancellation is a state-machine/process design flaw**: the enforcement action (cancel) removes the only tool (filing access) that would let the taxpayer self-cure, which is a backend workflow ordering bug, not a display bug.

## 4. What's already been tried, and why gaps remain

- **62 empanelled GST Suvidha Providers (GSPs)** — licensed intermediaries with API access to the portal — build filing software and automated reconciliation tools on top of GSTN ([Microvistatech GSP list](https://www.microvistatech.com/blog/gst-suvidha-providers-gsp-list-2025/); [Cleartax](https://cleartax.in/s/list-gst-suvidha-providers-gsp)).
- **CA-assisted filing** is near-universal for anything beyond the simplest return, because notice language and reconciliation logic are too technical for most traders to self-serve.
- **Accounting software (Tally, Zoho Books, ClearTax, Busy, etc.)** now ships built-in GSTR-2A/2B reconciliation tools that flag mismatches before filing ([SAG Infotech](https://blog.saginfotech.com/gst-software-resolves-gstr-3b-2a-2b-itc-mismatches)).
- **The gap that remains:** all of the above assume the business can afford a CA or a paid software subscription and has the compliance literacy to act on what the tool tells them. The very small trader — sole proprietor, single-GSTIN, no accounting staff — is the one who (a) can't afford ongoing CA retainer for every notice, (b) doesn't reconcile proactively so finds out about a mismatch only when a DRC notice lands with a fixed response deadline, and (c) is most exposed to suo moto cancellation because a missed filing snowballs silently for months before the cancellation notice arrives. This is the underserved segment the existing GSP/CA/software ecosystem does not reach, because none of those tools translate a specific received notice into "here is exactly what happened, here is the one number that's wrong, here is what to file and by when" for someone who isn't already tax-literate.

## 5. Buildathon fit assessment

Fully buildable in ~3 days with synthetic data only, and it maps directly onto the hard rules (no live systems, no undocumented private APIs, no real IDs/OTP/payment data):

- **Mock dataset needed:** synthetic GSTR-1 / GSTR-2A / GSTR-2B / GSTR-3B line-item records for a set of fictional GSTINs, with deliberately engineered mismatches (a supplier invoice missing from 2B, a GSTIN typo, a period mismatch, a duplicate claim) — each tagged with a "true root cause" so the prototype's diagnosis can be scored against ground truth.
- **Mock notice text:** synthetic DRC-01B/DRC-01C/Section 73 notice bodies, written in the department's actual legal register (public examples exist and are safe to imitate structurally without copying real taxpayer data), paired with the transaction data that produced them.
- **The valuable, demoable part is the reasoning layer**: given (a) a synthetic notice and (b) the underlying synthetic return/ledger data, the tool pinpoints the exact offending line item, explains in plain language why the system flagged it, tells the user whether it's their error or a supplier's, and drafts the correct response (DRC-03 acknowledgment-and-pay vs. DRC-06 reply-and-contest) with the deadline clearly stated. This does not require touching gst.gov.in at all — it's a self-contained diagnosis-and-drafting engine that could later plug into a real GSTIN's actual GSTR data via the taxpayer's own downloaded reports.

## 6. Candidate "fix the failing moment" problem statements

> **"Why did I get this notice, and what do I actually owe?"** A sole-proprietor trader in Surat gets a DRC-01C in their inbox: "ITC claimed in GSTR-3B exceeds ITC available in GSTR-2B." The notice cites the amount and the legal section, not the transaction. GST notices are written in language ordinary traders can't parse, and the department is now issuing these automatically for every GSTIN's GSTR-3B submission from FY2024-25 without any officer in the loop (Cygnet, Razorpay). Confusing which reply form to use — DRC-03 versus DRC-06 — "can cost a client significantly" (SSCOIndia). We are not rebuilding the notice engine, we are fixing the moment a citizen opens a legal document they cannot read: match the notice to the underlying (mock) ledger, name the exact invoice or supplier that caused the flag, tell them in plain language whether it's their mistake or a vendor's late filing, and generate the correctly-formatted response before the clock runs out. Strong buildathon fit: synthetic GSTR-1/2A/2B/3B records plus synthetic notice text are fully mockable, and the hard part — matching notice language to a specific transaction and picking the right response form — is exactly the reasoning layer a hackathon should build, not a screen redesign.

> **"My vendor's mistake is costing me my own tax credit."** ITC mismatch happens because "suppliers may delay uploading invoices or make errors in the GSTIN, invoice number, or tax amounts," and the buyer only finds out when their own claimed credit in GSTR-3B doesn't match what's in GSTR-2B — sometimes months later via a DRC-01C (IRIS MSME, TaxTMI on MSME suppliers). The core unfairness: a compliant small buyer loses cash because of someone else's filing failure, and today has no early-warning system, only a post-hoc notice. We are not rebuilding GSTN's matching engine, we are fixing the moment before it becomes a notice: a running reconciliation view that watches (mock) purchase records against (mock) supplier filing status and flags "this vendor hasn't filed yet, your credit on this invoice is at risk" weeks before the automated notice would land, plus a one-tap explainer of who is responsible and what the buyer can do (chase the vendor, reverse the credit proactively, or wait). Good buildathon fit: synthetic multi-GSTIN transaction graph (buyer + several suppliers with staggered filing status) demos cleanly and shows the systemic root cause — dependency on a third party's compliance — that a UI fix could never solve.

> **"Is my GST registration about to die, and can I still save it?"** Suo moto cancellation triggers after continuous non-filing (commonly six months), driven mostly by "lack of awareness or cash flow constraints" rather than fraud (Tax2win, GimBooks), and once cancelled the taxpayer "would not be allowed to file return or upload invoices" for that period — a process design that removes the taxpayer's only tool for self-curing before they even know they're in danger (TaxBuddy). Most small proprietors don't track their own filing streak against the cancellation threshold; the first they hear of it is a cancellation order. We are not touching the cancellation logic itself, we are fixing the moment before the cliff: a countdown that reads (mock) filing history, tells the trader in plain language how many months of non-filing they've accumulated and exactly what happens on month six, and walks them through the cheapest path back to compliant (which returns to file first, in what order, to stop the clock). Strong buildathon fit: entirely mockable from a synthetic filing-history table per GSTIN, and the demo payoff — catching someone two months before their business effectively loses its ability to legally invoice — is visceral and easy to show live.

> **"Will my registration get rejected for a reason I can fix in five minutes?"** The most frequent 2025-26 rejection trigger isn't fraud or ineligibility, it's a document-matching failure: "mismatch between the registered office address in REG-01 and the supporting document," or shared premises without a proper NOC (AABK). CBIC had to issue Instruction 03/2025-GST specifically telling officers to stop over-demanding irrelevant paperwork, an implicit admission the verification pipeline was inconsistent and slow, with waits of "7-10 days or more" before the 2025 fast-track reform (SSCOIndia, MyGSTRefund). We are not replacing the officer's verification, we are fixing the moment before submission: a pre-flight checker that takes the applicant's (mock) address proof, business type, and premises documents and runs the same matching logic officers use — flagging exactly which document is missing, mismatched, or needs a NOC — before the application goes in and burns a review cycle. Decent buildathon fit, though weaker as a demo story than the notice/ITC ideas above because the failure mode is more mundane (paperwork mismatch) and the "moment" being fixed is preventative rather than a dramatic rescue; still fully mockable from synthetic REG-01 applications and a synthetic rejection-reason ruleset.

> **"My goods are ready to ship, why won't the portal let me move them?"** E-way bill generation is blocked outright if GST returns are overdue, and separately by a 180-day rule barring e-way bills for invoices dated too far in the past, so goods can be packed and paid for with no legal way to move them (FilewithUs). Small transporters and traders without dedicated compliance staff are the ones most likely to hit this blind, discovering the block at the loading dock rather than in advance, and then facing detention and penalties for late or incorrect generation (GimBooks). We are not rebuilding the e-way bill portal, we are fixing the moment of dispatch: before goods leave the warehouse, a pre-check against (mock) filing status and invoice age tells the dispatcher in plain language whether the e-way bill will actually generate, and if not, exactly which return needs filing first to unblock it. Weakest of the five for buildathon narrative — it's a useful pre-flight tool but a less emotionally resonant "failing moment" than a notice or a dying registration, and the underlying rule logic (180-day cutoff, filing-status gate) is simple enough that the demo risks reading as a static checklist rather than a reasoning system.

---

### Recommendation

The strongest candidate is the **first one — decoding an automated GST notice against the underlying ledger and drafting the correct response.** It has the clearest "citizen hits a wall and needs an interpreter" shape, the richest synthetic dataset to build against (notice text + full GSTR-1/2A/2B/3B chain), and the most obvious backend/process fix: today the system auto-generates a legally binding notice with zero explanation layer, and the prototype supplies exactly that missing layer without touching any live government system.
