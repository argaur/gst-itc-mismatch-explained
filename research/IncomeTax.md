# Research: Income Tax e-Filing Portal (incometax.gov.in)

Pulled 2026-08-24. Secondary research only, no live portal access, no real PAN/OTP data used or proposed.

---

## 1. What the platform actually does

The Income Tax e-Filing portal (built and run by Infosys as "portal 2.0" since June 2021, replacing an earlier NSDL-era system) is the single entry point for:

- **ITR filing** — pre-filled forms (ITR-1 through ITR-7) drawing on Form 26AS, AIS/TIS and employer TDS data, e-verification via Aadhaar OTP/net banking/DSC.
- **Refund tracking** — a status tracker (Return Submitted → Verified → Processed → Refund Issued) tied to bank-account pre-validation.
- **AIS/TIS (Annual Information Statement / Taxpayer Information Summary)** — an aggregated feed of financial data pulled from banks, employers, brokers, mutual funds, registrars and other "reporting entities," with a feedback mechanism (correct / not fully correct / not correct / duplicate / relates to other person / denied) taxpayers can use to dispute individual entries. ([carajput.com](https://carajput.com/learn/ais-woes-how-taxpayers-are-struggling-with-inaccurate-reporting.html), [taxbuddy.com](https://www.taxbuddy.com/blog/ais-mismatch-and-tax-notice))
- **Automated notices/intimations** — Section 143(1) intimation (an automated post-processing computation sheet, not a "notice" in the legal-scrutiny sense, but frequently confused with one), Section 154 rectification orders, Section 148 reassessment notices, and outstanding-demand notices.
- **Rectification requests (Section 154)** — taxpayer-initiated correction of "mistakes apparent from record" in a 143(1) intimation or CPC/AO order. A newer feature lets taxpayers route rectification requests to their Assessing Officer (AO) directly online. ([blog.saginfotech.com](https://blog.saginfotech.com/it-dept-requests-ao-seeking-rectification-e-filing-portal), [incometax.gov.in](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/perform-rectification-faq))
- **Outstanding tax demand response** — taxpayers can "Agree" or "Disagree" with a demand and upload supporting documents. ([cleartax.in](https://cleartax.in/s/how-to-respond-to-demand-notice-from-income-tax-department), [incometaxindia.gov.in](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/tax-services/response-to-outstanding-tax-demand))
- **Grievance redressal** — "e-Nivaran" grievance module on the portal, escalating to CPGRAMS (pgportal.gov.in) and, in theory, an Income Tax Ombudsman if unresolved. Stated target: acknowledgment within 7 days, resolution within 30 days for simple cases, 60-90 days for cases needing inter-departmental coordination. ([righttoinformation.wiki](https://righttoinformation.wiki/income-tax-refund-notice-e-nivaran-cpgrams-india), [patronaccounting.com](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/income-tax-portal-grievance-and-compliance-handling-a-complete-guide))

## 2. Where it concretely fails users today

### a) Portal instability during filing season — a recurring, government-acknowledged failure
- At the June 2021 launch of portal 2.0, the Finance Ministry itself flagged that "even after 2.5 months since launch... glitches in the portal have not been resolved. In fact, since 21/08/2021 the portal itself is not available," and summoned Infosys CEO Salil Parekh to explain. Infosys had been paid ₹164.5 crore for the project as of that point. ([india.com](https://www.india.com/business/income-tax-portal-infosys-says-new-itr-website-live-finance-ministry-summons-infosys-ceo-top-points-4906368/), [deccanherald.com](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Ffinmin-summons-infosys-ceo-salil-parekh-to-explain-non-resolution-of-i-t-portal-glitches-1022345.html))
- This is not a one-off: for AY 2024-25 (filing season mid-2024), taxpayers again reported portal glitches close to the July 31 deadline, prompting Infosys to set up a dedicated "war room." ([business-standard.com, 17 Jul 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/itr-2024-taxpayers-struggle-with-glitches-on-i-t-portal-as-july-31-nears-124071700320_1.html), [deccanherald.com](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/infosys-sets-up-dedicated-war-room-for-i-t-portal-glitches-as-itr-filing-deadline-nears-1061830.html))
- For AY 2025-26, "huge traffic disrupted the e-filing portal, with many taxpayers unable to either log in or finding it slow," forcing CBDT to extend the deadline by a day to September 16, 2025 — the second deadline extension in as many years attributable to portal load. The Finance Ministry later confirmed penalties were levied on the managed service provider (Infosys) specifically for portal-caused extensions. ([upstox.com](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/tax/income-tax-return-itr-filing-last-date-extension-due-date-deadline-2025-live-updates/liveblog-181241/), [business-standard.com, 16 Sep 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/income-tax-return-deadline-extended-by-24-hours-after-portal-slows-down-125091600313_1.html))

### b) Refund delays and processing backlogs — large and measurable
- As of 4 February 2026, of 8,79,62,234 ITRs filed for AY 2025-26, **24,64,044 returns (roughly 2.8%) had been pending beyond 90 days.** ([upstox.com](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/latest-updates/income-tax-alert-24-64-lakh-returns-pending-as-8-80-crore-it-rs-filed-for-ay-2025-26/article-189238/))
- As of 24 March 2026, 8,89,20,822 returns had been filed, 8,77,86,233 verified, but only 8,50,59,270 processed — **over 27 lakh verified-but-unprocessed returns still sitting in the pipeline**, months after the filing deadline. ([angelone.in](https://www.angelone.in/news/taxation/itr-refund-delays-persist-in-fy-2025-26-over-27-lakh-returns-still-pending-processing))
- Legally, CPC has until 9 months after the end of the relevant financial year to process a return (i.e. until 31 December 2026 for AY 2025-26 returns) — meaning a taxpayer can be told nothing more than "wait," for well over a year after they filed, with no visibility into which internal step (validation, TDS reconciliation, bank-account verification, manual review) is holding up their specific return. ([upstox.com](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/latest-updates/income-tax-alert-24-64-lakh-returns-pending-as-8-80-crore-it-rs-filed-for-ay-2025-26/article-189238/))
- Common causes cited: unvalidated/incorrect bank account details, PAN-bank account linkage not done, outstanding prior-year demand silently offset against the refund, and TDS/AIS mismatches triggering manual review. ([bajajfinserv.in](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/income-tax-return-not-received-on-itr), [poonawallafincorp.com](https://poonawallafincorp.com/blogs/tax/itr-processed-but-refund-not-credited-bank-account))

### c) AIS/TIS mismatches generating automated notices at scale
- **In AY 2025-26, 42 lakh taxpayers received AIS-ITR mismatch notices**, with the most common triggers being unreported bank FD interest and dividend income below ₹5,000 (income the taxpayer often didn't even know was being tracked, since small dividend/interest amounts are easy to overlook). This is the single clearest quantified "backend logic hitting citizens at scale" data point found in this research. ([ampcome.com](https://www.ampcome.com/post/ai-tools-for-tax-professionals-in-india) — figure surfaced via aggregated search; corroborated qualitatively by [carajput.com](https://carajput.com/learn/ais-woes-how-taxpayers-are-struggling-with-inaccurate-reporting.html) and [taxbuddy.com](https://www.taxbuddy.com/blog/ais-mismatch-and-tax-notice))
- Root causes of AIS data being wrong are structural, not user error: banks report on accrual basis while individuals often think in receipt-basis terms; the *same* interest gets double-counted when both a bank branch and its central processing unit report it, or when an old and new bank both report after a merger; and transactions get mis-attributed to the wrong PAN entirely (common with joint accounts, joint property purchases, or a company's transaction tagged to a director's personal PAN). ([carajput.com](https://carajput.com/learn/ais-woes-how-taxpayers-are-struggling-with-inaccurate-reporting.html))
- The correction path is not something the taxpayer can resolve on the portal at all — they must contact the reporting entity (bank branch, employer HR, broker) and get *them* to file a correction return; only then does AIS update, and only in the next processing cycle. In the meantime, if the ITR doesn't match AIS, the automated 143(1) process may add the disputed amount as a demand plus interest. ([carajput.com](https://carajput.com/learn/ais-woes-how-taxpayers-are-struggling-with-inaccurate-reporting.html))
- Budget 2025 widened the scope of what CPC can flag during processing — it can now consider inconsistencies against *prior-year* return information too, not just the current year — meaning more automated mismatches are likely, not fewer. ([indiatax.ai](https://indiatax.ai/blog/ais-mismatch-income-tax-notice), search summary corroborating Budget 2025 CPC scope change)

### d) Confusing/wrong demand notices — a specific, documented 2025 incident
- The IT Department sent demand notices to a large number of taxpayers — mostly those with total income below ₹7 lakh — who had revised their returns in January 2025 to claim the Section 87A rebate after a Bombay High Court order required the department to allow that revision window. Despite the court order, the department's system continued to deny the rebate specifically on short-term capital gains taxed at special rates, generating demand notices the taxpayers believed were simply wrong. Tax professionals ("the tax fraternity") had to formally petition authorities to fix this. ([business-standard.com](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/cas-taxpayers-push-back-after-income-tax-dept-sends-rebate-notices-125040400618_1.html))
- This is a clean example of a **backend rule-engine bug** (rebate-eligibility logic not updated to match a court-mandated exception) surfacing to citizens as an opaque, wrong, legally-worded demand notice with no plain-language explanation of why it was issued or that it might be erroneous.

### e) Grievance redressal is slow and multi-hop
- e-Nivaran grievances target 7-day acknowledgment / 30-day resolution, but the same source notes complex cases (refund reprocessing, demand correction, cases needing inter-departmental coordination) can take 60-90 days, with CPGRAMS and an Income Tax Ombudsman as further, higher-friction escalation layers a citizen has to discover and pursue themselves. ([righttoinformation.wiki](https://righttoinformation.wiki/income-tax-refund-notice-e-nivaran-cpgrams-india), [patronaccounting.com](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/income-tax-portal-grievance-and-compliance-handling-a-complete-guide))

## 3. Root causes: UI-level vs. backend/process-level

The hackathon brief wants backend/process fixes, not reskins. On the evidence gathered, the pain is overwhelmingly **backend/process**, not screen design:

| Pain point | Root cause layer |
|---|---|
| Portal downtime at deadline | Infrastructure capacity/load-testing under a managed-service contract, not UI |
| Refund stuck 90+ days with no reason shown | Internal processing pipeline (validation → TDS reconciliation → bank verification → manual review) has no citizen-visible state machine; the tracker shows 4 coarse stages but not *why* a specific return is stuck at one |
| AIS mismatch notices (42 lakh in one year) | Third-party reporting-entity data quality (banks/employers/brokers) feeding an automated rule engine with no reconciliation or confidence-scoring step before it triggers a legal-sounding notice |
| Wrong 87A rebate demand notices | Rule engine not updated to reflect a court-ordered exception — a process/logic bug, not a display bug |
| AIS correction requires going back to the reporting entity | Structural: the portal has no dispute-resolution or reconciliation workflow of its own; it just displays what reporting entities sent, and the correction loop runs *outside* the portal entirely |
| Grievance escalation ladder (e-Nivaran → CPGRAMS → Ombudsman) | Process fragmentation across systems the citizen has to discover and re-explain their issue to at each hop |

The one clear common denominator across the accessible pain points: **the portal explains "what" (a status, a number, a demand amount) but never "why," and never "what happens next and by when."** That gap is a genuine backend/process problem — it requires reasoning over the multiple systems and data sources (AIS entries, TDS credit, prior-year demand, bank validation status, processing stage) that feed each citizen-facing artifact, and today no layer does that reasoning for the citizen.

## 4. What's already been tried, and why the gap persists

- **ClearTax and similar filing intermediaries** (TaxBuddy, and various new AI-branded tools like IndiaTax.AI, TaxNoticeAI) already offer AI-assisted filing, AIS/26AS prefill, and paid "notice management" services where a human/AI-assisted team drafts a response to a received notice. ([cleartax.in](https://cleartax.in/services/tax-notice-management-plan/p), [ampcome.com](https://www.ampcome.com/post/ai-tools-for-tax-professionals-in-india))
- These tools are almost entirely **filing-side and paid-response-side**: they help a taxpayer file more accurately, or (for a fee, often requiring a CA) help draft a reply *after* a notice or demand has already landed. None of the tools surfaced in this research proactively catches an AIS mismatch or a refund-stuck condition *before* it becomes a notice, explains a specific already-issued notice in plain language for free at the moment of confusion, or diagnoses which of the ~5-6 known backend failure categories (bank data error, PAN mis-attribution, double-counting, rebate-logic bug, bank-validation failure) is behind a specific citizen's problem.
- The gap exists because the reasoning layer needed (cross-referencing AIS entries against Form 26AS/16, prior-year returns, and known bug patterns like the 87A rebate issue) requires structured access to the *content* of a notice/AIS statement, not just a filing wizard — and today that reasoning is manual, paid, and CA-mediated, not something a citizen holding a confusing PDF can get answered instantly and free.

## 5. Buildathon fit assessment

**Yes, this is prototypable in ~3 days on mock/synthetic data only, with no live portal access, no real PAN, and no scraping.**

Needed synthetic datasets, all fully fabricable:
1. **Synthetic AIS/TIS records** — a small set of mock taxpayer profiles, each with fabricated bank interest, dividend, and TDS entries, including a few "planted" errors (double-counted interest, wrong-PAN attribution, accrual-vs-receipt mismatch) modeled on the real failure patterns documented above.
2. **Synthetic Form 26AS / Form 16** for the same mock profiles, to demonstrate the reconciliation logic.
3. **Synthetic 143(1) intimation / demand notice text** — a handful of realistic notice templates (including one modeled on the real 87A-rebate-denial bug) with the legal boilerplate that real citizens find confusing.
4. **Synthetic refund-status timeline** — a mock internal pipeline (filed → verified → TDS reconciled → bank validated → processed → refund issued/failed) with per-stage timestamps and failure reasons, to power a "why is my refund stuck" diagnostic.
5. **A small rules/knowledge base** of the ~5-6 known failure categories (bank-account not pre-validated, AIS/26AS mismatch, outstanding prior-year demand adjusted against refund, rebate/regime rule bug, PAN mis-attribution, manual scrutiny hold) to drive an LLM-based diagnosis-and-plain-language-explanation layer — this is the actual "backend fix," reasoning over data the portal already has but never explains.

No live scraping, no real Aadhaar/PAN/OTP, no payment data required anywhere in this build.

## 6. Problem statement candidates

> **"Why does my AIS say I earned interest twice?"** A taxpayer opens their Annual Information Statement before filing and finds the same fixed-deposit interest listed twice — once reported by the bank branch, once by the bank's central processing unit, or once by an old bank and once by the new bank after a merger. Left uncorrected, this doubled figure flows straight into the automated 143(1) reconciliation and can trigger a demand notice for tax on income the taxpayer never actually earned. In AY 2025-26 alone, 42 lakh taxpayers received AIS-ITR mismatch notices, with FD interest and small dividend income the leading triggers. Today the only fix is to leave the portal entirely, track down the reporting bank branch, and get them to file a correction return in a future cycle — the portal displays the error but has no way to resolve it. Strong buildathon fit: a mock AIS feed with a handful of planted double-counting, wrong-basis, and wrong-PAN entries is fully synthetic, and the valuable part is a reconciliation engine that cross-checks AIS against a mock Form 26AS/16 and flags exactly which entry is duplicated or misattributed and why, in plain language, before the taxpayer ever files — turning a downstream demand notice into a pre-filing catch.

> **"I got a demand notice for a rebate the court said I could claim."** In early 2025, taxpayers who revised their returns before 15 January to claim the Section 87A rebate — after a Bombay High Court order explicitly required the department to allow that revision window — started receiving demand notices anyway, because the department's rule engine kept denying the rebate specifically on short-term capital gains taxed at special rates. Tax professionals had to formally push back before the issue was acknowledged. The notice itself gives the taxpayer no indication that the underlying rule might simply be wrong, or that a court order already covers their exact situation — it just states a tax liability in legal boilerplate. This is a clean instance of a backend rule bug surfacing as a citizen-facing legal document with zero context. Strong buildathon fit: build a synthetic notice generator with a handful of realistic 143(1)/demand templates (including one modeled on the 87A case), and a diagnosis layer that checks a notice's stated reason against a small mock rule-and-precedent base to tell the taxpayer "this notice may be based on an outdated rule — here is the exact court order/circular that applies, and here is the rectification request text to file," rather than leaving them to consult a CA cold.

> **"Where is my refund, and what stage is it actually stuck at?"** As of February 2026, roughly 24.6 lakh returns for AY 2025-26 had been pending beyond 90 days out of 8.8 crore filed; a month later over 27 lakh verified returns were still unprocessed. CPC legally has until nine months after the financial year ends to process a return, so a citizen can be told nothing more than "processing" for well over a year. The public tracker shows four coarse states (submitted / verified / processed / refund issued) with no visibility into which of the actual internal steps — TDS credit reconciliation, bank account pre-validation, an outstanding prior-year demand silently netted off, or a manual scrutiny hold — is the one holding up their specific refund. We are not rebuilding the CPC processing pipeline, we are fixing the moment a citizen checks status for the fifth time and still has no idea what to do next: given a mock internal pipeline state and the citizen's own mock financial profile, tell them in plain language which specific step is blocking them and the exact action (validate bank account, respond to demand, wait for manual review with an expected date) that unblocks it. Good buildathon fit: the internal pipeline can be a fully synthetic state machine with per-stage timestamps and canned failure reasons drawn from the documented real causes (unvalidated bank account, AIS mismatch, prior-year demand offset), and the valuable part is translating pipeline state into an actionable next step instead of a static label.

> **"Is this actually a scary legal notice, or just a computer-generated adjustment sheet?"** Section 143(1) intimations are automated processing outputs — arithmetic corrections, incorrect-claim flags, AIS/26AS mismatches — but taxpayers routinely confuse them with a genuine notice initiating scrutiny or prosecution, because both arrive as dense legal-format PDFs with section numbers and demand figures. That confusion drives people either into panic (paying a demand they could have disputed) or into a paid CA consultation for something a plain-language read could have resolved in two minutes. Budget 2025 widened what CPC can flag during automated processing to include prior-year inconsistencies too, so this volume of automated correspondence is set to grow, not shrink. We are not rebuilding notice generation, we are fixing the moment a citizen opens a notice PDF and doesn't know if it's routine or urgent: parse a mock notice, classify it against the known categories (routine 143(1) adjustment vs. AIS mismatch vs. rectification order vs. genuine demand vs. reassessment notice under 148), and hand back a plain-language summary of what happened, whether it's actionable, and the exact next step and deadline. Strong buildathon fit: a small library of synthetic notice templates covering the real categories above is enough to demo the classification-and-explanation layer end to end, with no real notice data needed.

> **"My bank reported my interest wrong, and I have to prove it to a government computer."** When a reporting entity (bank, broker, employer) sends the tax department incorrect or duplicated data, the taxpayer's only recourse is the AIS feedback mechanism — six categories (correct / not fully correct / not correct / duplicate / relates to other person / denied) that record a dispute but do not resolve it; resolution requires the *reporting entity* to file its own correction, on its own schedule, outside the taxpayer's control or visibility entirely. In the meantime the department still treats the unresolved AIS entry as ground truth for 143(1) processing. This is the least "designed for the citizen" workflow on the whole portal: the person with the least power (the individual taxpayer) is asked to resolve an error made by the entity with the most power (the bank), through a form that just records a complaint into a void. Medium buildathon fit: fully mockable with synthetic AIS entries and a synthetic "reporting entity correction" queue, but the demo needs to make clear it's helping the taxpayer build an evidence-backed dispute packet (bank statement vs. AIS entry, side by side, with the specific discrepancy type identified) rather than pretending to fix the reporting entity's system itself — otherwise it risks looking like a reskin of the existing feedback form rather than a process fix.

## Notes on evidence quality

- The "42 lakh AIS-ITR mismatch notices" figure for AY 2025-26 is the strongest single quantified statistic found for the notice-mismatch problem; it surfaced via an aggregated search-result summary rather than being independently confirmed by fetching the primary source page — **flagged as needing a direct-source check** (likely a CBDT press statement, Economic Times, or Livemint article) before quoting it in a pitch deck without a live citation.
- Refund-pending figures (24.64 lakh / 27 lakh) are sourced from Upstox/AngelOne aggregation of what reads as CBDT/parliamentary data as of specific 2026 dates — solid, but worth a direct CBDT/PIB citation if used in the final submission.
- Portal downtime and deadline-extension history (2021, 2024, 2025) is corroborated across multiple independent outlets (Business Standard, Deccan Herald, Upstox, India.com, The Wire) and is high-confidence.
- The Section 87A rebate demand-notice episode is corroborated by a single Business Standard piece; strong and specific, but only one outlet found in this pass — treat as accurate but not multiply-sourced.
- AIS structural error causes (double-counting, accrual/receipt mismatch, wrong-PAN attribution) are consistent across several tax-advisory blogs (carajput.com, taxbuddy.com) but these are practitioner explainer sites, not primary regulatory sources — directionally reliable, not statistically quantified beyond the 42-lakh figure above.
