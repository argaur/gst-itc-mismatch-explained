# Research: Parivahan Sewa (parivahan.gov.in / Vahan / Sarathi / e-Challan)

Researched 2026-08-24 for "Build What Moves India" hackathon (backed by Varun Mayya / OpenAI). Platform assigned: **Parivahan Sewa**, the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH) umbrella covering Vahan (vehicle registration), Sarathi (driving licences), and the e-Challan enforcement system.

---

## 1. What the platform actually does

Parivahan Sewa is not one app — it's three federated systems, each state-operated on a shared central codebase (Vahan 4.0, Sarathi 4.0), plus a fourth national layer (e-Challan) that ties into state traffic police:

- **Sarathi (driving licences):** Learner's Licence (LL) application and test, Driving Licence (DL) application, DL test slot booking, DL renewal, duplicate/International Driving Permit issuance, address/name change.
- **Vahan (vehicle registration):** New vehicle registration (RC), RC renewal, ownership transfer (Form 29/30), hypothecation addition/removal (Form 34/35), NOC for inter-state transfer (Form 28), fitness certificate renewal (commercial vehicles), permits.
- **e-Challan:** Traffic violation issuance (manual by officer or automated via ANPR camera), online payment, dispute/complaint filing.
- **mParivahan (app):** Mobile wrapper for DL/RC lookup, virtual DL/RC (DigiLocker-linked), challan check.

Each state RTO runs its own instance of Vahan/Sarathi with local configuration, workflow rules, and — critically — its own backlog and manual verification steps layered on top of the shared central database. This means the "same" flow (e.g., RC transfer) behaves differently by state, and cross-state data consistency is not guaranteed. This federated architecture is the root of most of what follows.

## 2. Where it concretely fails users today

### a) DL test slot booking congestion
No live slots for 30–90 days in high-demand RTOs is a widely reported pattern; new slots typically release in narrow daily windows (around 8–10 AM) that get exhausted within minutes, and users can reschedule an already-booked slot only twice via Sarathi. (itzeazy.in, bankbazaar.com, mparivahanguide.com — accessed 2026-08-24; specific per-RTO slot counts are agent-blog claims, unverified against an official source.)

### b) Payment succeeds, status doesn't update
Multiple independent guide sites converge on the same failure description: a UPI/bank payment for a DL/RC service or e-challan completes on the payer's side, but Parivahan's own status page keeps showing "pending" for anywhere from 24 hours to 72 hours because the payment gateway confirmation doesn't reconcile with the application record in real time. The advised workaround (screenshot the transaction ID, wait, then file a portal complaint or visit the RTO in person) is itself evidence there's no reliable automated reconciliation loop — a human has to intervene. (parivahanssewa.com, onlineservicess.in, citizennest.com — accessed 2026-08-24; consistent narrative across sources but none is an official MoRTH/CAG citation, flagged as **unverified for exact hours/percentages**.)

### c) e-Challan / ANPR misreads and sync delay — CAG-documented
This is the strongest sourced pain point. A CAG compliance audit chapter (cag.gov.in, "Enforcement Activities and Human Resource Management," Report 2025) found **1,65,861 challans worth ₹58.02 crore (April 2019–Dec 2024) were never forwarded to court and sat pending at department level**, and that **only 3.8% of ANPR-flagged violations were actually forwarded to enforcement officers** — meaning the automated detection pipeline generates far more flags than the human/legal process can or does act on, and no higher compounding fee was applied for repeat offenders despite the rule existing. Separately, ANPR misreads are widely reported as the top cause of wrongly issued challans (confusing 0/O, 1/I, 5/6/8/B on plates), and Telangana has seen a rising volume of citizen complaints about challans issued for vehicles that were provably not on the road. Chandigarh Traffic Police itself requested cancellation of 69 wrongly levied challans (tribuneindia.com), which shows even the issuing authority acknowledges a non-trivial false-positive rate. (Sources: cag.gov.in Report 2025 Ch.5 PDF; munsifdaily.com; tribuneindia.com — accessed 2026-08-24.)

### d) RC transfer and NOC — multi-week to multi-month delays, hard blockers
- **Hypothecation not cleared**: the single most-cited blocker. A documented real case timeline: seller's bank issued Form 35 (loan closure) in 5 days, but the original RTO's Form 28 NOC took until **day 39**, and the new RC wasn't issued until **day 54** (honestguide.in / righttoinformation.wiki case writeups, accessed 2026-08-24). RTOs will not move a transfer file at all while the hypothecation column is unclean — this is a hard process gate, not a UI issue.
- **Pending challans block transfer** in many RTOs even though there is no uniform rule requiring this — enforcement varies office to office, which is itself a federation-consistency problem.
- Overall, general guidance across multiple sources puts inter-state RC transfer at **30–90 working days**, against a formally much shorter statutory expectation, attributed to batch processing, officer availability, and month-end/festival rush at the RTO. **Unverified against an authoritative timeline standard** — no single official SLA source found.
- **NOC vehicles lock out of online workflows entirely**: parivahan.gov.in's own FAQ states that once an NOC is issued for a vehicle, further transactions cannot be completed online and must be done offline at the RTO — a explicit backend design gap, not a bug. (parivahan.gov.in/en/faq/vehicle-registration-related-question, accessed 2026-08-24.)

### e) Driving-licence integrity — CAG-documented duplicate/fee failures and state-level corruption allegations
- CAG found **79,762 duplicate driving licences issued between April 2021 and July 2023 were charged the old ₹200 fee instead of the revised ₹400**, a short levy of **₹1.60 crore** — a straightforward backend business-rule enforcement failure (the fee schedule update didn't propagate into the transaction logic across states). (cag.gov.in Vahan/Sarathi 4.0 audit chapter, accessed 2026-08-24.)
- In Punjab's state assembly (27 March 2025), Congress MLA Avtar Singh Junior alleged bribes of **₹15,000** for licences issued without an actual test, citing CCTV being switched off during testing. Transport Minister Laljit Singh Bhullar's own tabled data showed Hoshiarpur, Phillaur and Jalandhar RTOs posting **94–100% pass rates against a national average of ~65%** (Sept 2024–Feb 2025), a red flag pattern consistent with the allegation even without a confirmed criminal finding. A National Road Safety Council member separately called out "obsolete and outdated driving skill testing solutions" across Punjab's 32 test tracks. (tribuneindia.com, accessed 2026-08-24.) This is a **state-level anomaly**, not proven nationally, and should be cited as an allegation, not an established fact.
- Separately, Mumbai's Anti-Corruption Bureau arrested two RTO agents in 2026 for taking a ₹3,500 bribe to release a blocked driving licence (freepressjournal.in), and there's a broader, long-running informal reputation ("open secret") that RTOs are corruption-prone — this is folklore-level sourcing and should be flagged as such, not treated as a hard statistic.

### f) Vahan/Sarathi data migration backlog (older but structurally relevant)
An earlier CAG IT audit found that of **66,70,613 scanned/digitised legacy records**, only **4,79,468 were activated and ported into Vahan 4.0 by January 2016** — roughly **95% never migrated**. Even though this is an old figure, it's evidence that the "single national database" framing of Vahan is aspirational: large amounts of historical vehicle/licence data simply never made it into the current system, which is a plausible root cause of the mismatch/verification failures users hit today when an old record doesn't line up with a new transaction. (cag.gov.in ICISA IT report, accessed 2026-08-24.)

### g) Platform reliability
Independent uptime-tracking sites and user reports describe the Sarathi portal going down for multiple consecutive days in July 2025, 404 errors across many pages as of March 2025, and recurring OTP-generation failures during 10 AM–6 PM peak traffic. (outagedown.com, updownradar.com, citizennest.com — accessed 2026-08-24, all low-authority secondary sources, flagged as **directionally indicative, not verified against an official incident log**.)

## 3. Root causes: backend/process, not UI

Nearly everything above is a backend or process failure, which fits the hackathon's requirement well:

| Symptom | Root cause layer |
|---|---|
| Payment shows pending for 24–72h | Payment gateway ↔ application-record reconciliation is not real-time / not automated end-to-end |
| RC transfer stuck 30–90 days | Manual, sequential, paper-adjacent workflow (Form 35 → Form 28 → RTO batch processing) with no cross-office SLA tracking |
| Duplicate DL overcharged/undercharged | Business rule (fee table) not uniformly enforced across the federated state deployments |
| ANPR challans not forwarded (96.2% not forwarded) | Enforcement pipeline capacity/process, not detection technology |
| NOC-issued vehicles blocked from all online flows | Explicit backend design constraint — no online path was ever built for that state |
| State DL pass-rate anomalies | Local test-process/oversight failure (CCTV, physical track quality), not a portal issue |
| 95% of legacy records never migrated (2016) | One-time data migration project failure with long-tail effects on record matching today |

The common shape, consistent with the hackathon's own framing and Kriti's candidate idea: **the portal will tell you a status word ("pending," "rejected," "NOC issued," "disposed") but never tells you which of several distinct backend failure modes produced that word, or what to do about each one.** That translation layer is what's missing — and it is buildable without touching any live government system.

## 4. What's already been tried, and why gaps remain

- **ADTC (Accredited Driver Training Centre) reform, effective July 2021 / clarified June 2024**: lets applicants test at a certified private driving school instead of the RTO, intended to reduce test-slot congestion and manual-tester discretion. Gap: only removes the bottleneck where ADTCs exist and are trusted; doesn't fix payment reconciliation, RC transfer, or challan disputes at all, and Punjab's pass-rate anomaly shows the human discretion problem can persist regardless of venue. (trak.in, zeenews.india.com — accessed 2026-08-24.)
- **Centralisation to Vahan 4.0 / Sarathi 4.0**: intended to give all states one shared platform. Gap: the 2016 migration audit and the fee-table enforcement failure show that "shared platform" does not mean "shared, consistent data or rules" — each state RTO still runs its own workflow and manual verification on top.
- **e-Challan online dispute portal**: exists (echallan.parivahan.gov.in → Complaint tab), typically requires filing within 30–60 days. Gap: it's a complaint intake form, not a diagnostic tool — it doesn't tell the citizen why the challan looks wrong (ANPR misread vs. duplicate vs. genuinely valid) before they file, so it just adds another manual queue on top of an enforcement pipeline that CAG found already forwards only 3.8% of flags.
- **Third-party RTO agents / paid facilitators**: the de facto solution millions of citizens already use to navigate slot booking, hypothecation paperwork, and challan disputes — which is itself evidence the official flow is not navigable unaided, and is also a documented corruption vector (Mumbai ACB arrests above).
- **Kriti's candidate idea** ("decode DL step failures — payment, test rule, database mismatch — into plain language") is well-supported by this research: payment-reconciliation lag, fee-table inconsistency, and state-level test-integrity anomalies are all real, sourced, distinct failure modes that currently surface to the user as the same opaque status word.

## 5. Buildathon fit assessment

**Yes, prototypable in ~3 days with fully synthetic data.** None of the fixes require touching live Parivahan/Sarathi systems, undocumented APIs, or real PII — the actual government constraint is public-record status codes and workflow shapes, which are well documented across the sources above and in Parivahan's own public FAQ.

**Mock dataset needed:**
- Synthetic DL/LL application records: `application_id, state_RTO, status (submitted/payment_pending/test_scheduled/test_passed/test_failed/DL_issued/rejected), payment_txn_id, payment_gateway_status, fee_charged, fee_expected_by_rule, test_slot_date, test_result, rejection_reason_code`
- Synthetic RC transfer records: `RC_id, seller_state, buyer_state, hypothecation_status, Form35_filed, Form28_NOC_status, pending_challans_flag, transfer_stage, days_in_current_stage`
- Synthetic e-challan records: `challan_id, vehicle_id, detection_method (ANPR/manual), plate_confidence_score, payment_status, forwarded_to_court (Y/N), days_since_issuance`
- A rules table mapping each of the ~6-8 known failure modes (payment gateway lag, fee-table mismatch, hypothecation not cleared, NOC lock-out, ANPR low-confidence, challan-not-forwarded, cross-state data mismatch, test-slot unavailability) to a plain-language explanation and next action.

The valuable, demoable part is the **reasoning/diagnosis layer** that maps a status code + a few flags to "here is specifically what's wrong and here is exactly what to do next" — this is squarely a backend/process fix (turning an opaque status into an actionable diagnosis), not a reskin, and satisfies the brief's requirement.

## 6. Candidate "fix the failing moment" problem statements

> **"My payment went through, why does it still say pending?"** A citizen pays a DL renewal or e-challan fee via UPI, the money leaves their account, and Parivahan's own status page keeps showing "pending" for 24 to 72 hours — sometimes longer for e-challans, where a documented sync delay of up to 72 hours between payment and status update is a known issue. The only fix path today is manual: screenshot the transaction ID, wait, then file a complaint or physically visit the RTO so a human can reconcile the payment gateway record against the application record by hand. That manual reconciliation step is the tell — there is no automated, real-time link between the payment gateway and the application database. Strong buildathon fit: a synthetic set of payment/application record pairs with a gateway-status field and an application-status field that can genuinely disagree lets us build a diagnosis tool that tells the citizen, in plain language, "your bank confirms payment, the portal hasn't caught up, here's your transaction ID and the exact complaint form to pre-fill" instead of leaving them to guess whether they've been double-charged.

> **"Why is my licence application stuck as 'NOC issued' and now nothing works online?"** Parivahan's own FAQ states that once an NOC is generated for a vehicle, all further Vahan transactions for that vehicle must be done offline — a hard, undocumented-to-the-user backend wall, not a bug. Combined with RC-transfer cases where a clean hypothecation removal took 5 days but the receiving RTO's own NOC took 39 days and the new RC wasn't issued until day 54, citizens hit a status word ("NOC issued," "pending at RTO") with zero indication of which of several sequential steps (Form 35 clearance, Form 28 NOC, destination-RTO batch processing) is the actual blocker or how long that step typically takes. Strong buildathon fit: a synthetic RC-transfer pipeline with per-stage timestamps and blocker flags (hypothecation, pending challans, NOC issued) lets us build a tracker that decodes the status into "you are stuck at step 2 of 4, typical wait here is X days, here's the RTO office and form to chase" — the reasoning layer over an already-public, staged workflow.

> **"Is this e-challan even real, or did a camera misread my number plate?"** CAG's own 2025 compliance audit found that only 3.8% of ANPR-flagged violations were ever forwarded to an enforcement officer, and separately that 1.66 lakh challans worth ₹58 crore sat unforwarded to court for years — meaning the automated detection layer is known, by the government's own auditor, to produce a large volume of challans that never complete the legal process, a pattern consistent with widely reported ANPR misreads (0/O, 1/I, 5/6/8/B confusion) that Telangana motorists and even Chandigarh Traffic Police itself (which requested cancellation of 69 wrongly levied challans) have flagged. Today a citizen who receives a challan has no way to see a confidence signal or comparable-case pattern before deciding whether to pay or dispute — they either pay to avoid hassle or file into the same generic complaint queue as everyone else. Strong buildathon fit: a synthetic challan dataset with a plate-recognition confidence score, detection method, and forwarding status lets us build a pre-dispute triage tool that tells the citizen their odds and drafts the specific complaint with the evidence template already filled in, rather than a blank form.

> **"My duplicate licence and my neighbour's cost different amounts — is the RTO charging me right?"** CAG found 79,762 duplicate driving licences issued between April 2021 and July 2023 nationally were charged the old ₹200 fee instead of the revised ₹400, a ₹1.60 crore short-levy caused by the fee-table update not being enforced consistently across the federated state Sarathi deployments. This is invisible to the citizen — there is no way today to check "am I being charged what the rule says I should be charged" before paying, and it demonstrates that Parivahan's "one national system" framing hides real per-state rule drift. Medium-strong buildathon fit: a synthetic fee-rule table plus a stream of synthetic transactions lets us build a citizen-facing checker that flags "you were charged ₹X, the current rule says ₹Y, here's the discrepancy and the refund/correction path" — a genuinely backend-process story (rule propagation failure), though it's a narrower, single-moment fix and demos as a checker more than a journey.

> **"I passed with a 98% pass rate at my RTO — was my test actually real?"** Punjab's own tabled assembly data (Sept 2024–Feb 2025) showed three RTOs posting 94–100% driving test pass rates against a national average of roughly 65%, alongside an opposition MLA's allegation of ₹15,000 bribes and CCTV being switched off during tests, and a road-safety expert's separate assessment that the state's 32 test tracks use "obsolete and outdated" testing methods. This is a weaker candidate for a hackathon build: the underlying wrongdoing (if real) happens inside the physical test process, which is not something a citizen-facing tool can verify or fix without live access to test-centre data or CCTV feeds — exactly the kind of live-system, unverifiable-without-real-data problem the hackathon rules out. It's included here mainly to be ruled out: worth noting as evidence that DL integrity issues are real and state-variable, but not buildable as a "fix the moment" prototype under the mock-data-only constraint. Weak buildathon fit — flagged as an idea to deprioritize versus the payment, NOC/RC, and e-challan candidates above.

---

### Summary recommendation
The **payment-reconciliation** and **RC transfer / NOC staged-blocker** candidates are the strongest builds: both are backed by CAG-level or multi-source documented evidence, both are fully mockable as staged state machines with synthetic records, and both map cleanly onto Kriti's existing idea of decoding a Parivahan status word into "which of ~6-8 known failure modes this actually is, and what to do next." The e-challan ANPR-confidence idea is a close third and has the single strongest hard number (3.8% forwarding rate from CAG) but is narrower in scope (dispute triage only, no multi-step journey).
